# Start the day on a known look

Casino and resort floors rely on A/V to set the opening environment across displays, sources, and audio zones. But every shift leaves decisions behind: a source changed for an event, a volume raised for a busy room, or a display left on after the audience moved on.

By morning, the floor may still reflect the night before instead of the day ahead. Property A/V and operations teams must determine what changed, what should still be running, and what needs to return to normal. Opening becomes a negotiation with the last shift.

A morning reset in current LUCI gives the property a known starting point. The property defines its opening state based on how the floor should operate, then saves that state as a preset. One command can restore:

- the intended sources;
- the selected display looks;
- the correct audio zones; and
- the opening volume levels.

The reset runs from the same interface staff use to operate the property’s A/V. They do not have to inspect every display, rebuild each zone, or retrace the previous shift’s changes across separate controls.

The move is direct:

1. Staff choose the opening preset.
2. They apply it.
3. LUCI returns the floor to the property’s selected state.

Displays and audio do not become separate reset jobs. The command reaches the systems already coordinated through LUCI.

That changes the opening conversation. Staff can verify that the floor matches the property’s decision instead of asking what the last shift changed. A known state also gives managers a clear standard across teams: the opening look belongs to the operation, not to one person’s memory.

Opening is not the only time that standard matters. Events, promotions, and late changes can move a room away from its normal look. After the event ends, staff need a defined point to return to. The same reset gives them that point without turning the close of one event into the manual setup for the next shift.

The result is consistency across shifts. The property sets the opening state; the last person who touched the controls does not.

LUCI works with and absorbs the A/V already on the floor. It does not replace the displays, audio, sources, or control systems the property has chosen. LUCI brings their controls into one operating interface, so a morning reset can act across the room instead of stopping at system boundaries.

A walkthrough starts with the property as it exists:

- the sources and zones staff operate now;
- the look the property wants at open;
- the changes that tend to remain after events or late shifts; and
- the systems staff currently reset one by one.

The LUCI team maps those conditions, defines what belongs in the opening state, and shows how staff can call it back from one interface.
