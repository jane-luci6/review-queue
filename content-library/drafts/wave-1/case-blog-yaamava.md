# Yaamava’: everything on the property, one interface

“One interface” only matters when the scope behind it is clear. Without a denominator, it can describe a single room, a single system, or a demonstration that never reaches the floor.

At Yaamava’ Resort & Casino in Highland, California, the denominator is the property: broadcast content, signage, audio, data, and displays on one platform. Thirty-four LED walls across seven venues operate inside that same interface. The resort covers 290,000 square feet.

Those facts establish what the phrase means here. One interface is not a control for one category of A/V. It is the operating point for systems that would otherwise remain separate across the resort.

The LED walls make the scale visible, but they are not the whole story. They sit alongside broadcast content, signage, audio, data, and displays in the same operating picture. The property does not need one platform for the walls and another for everything around them.

LUCI works with and absorbs those systems. It does not replace them. The A/V selected for the property remains in place; LUCI gives it one interface for control, automation, and execution.

For property A/V, IT, and operations leaders, that distinction is the reason to look past the phrase and into the case. Whole-resort control is not proven by a claim about simplicity. It is proven by the systems and venues that actually operate through the interface.

The categories matter as much as the count. Broadcast content, signage, audio, data, and displays do different work across a resort. Bringing them into one interface does not erase those differences. It gives the property one place to operate across them.

The interface carries the full scope, not a demonstration subset.

Yaamava’ provides that proof in public: 34 LED walls, seven venues, multiple categories of A/V and content, and a 290,000-square-foot resort run from a single interface. The numbers come before the benefit because they show what “one” is responsible for.

The live case study carries the full account. This is the door to it. If your property has accumulated separate systems for broadcast, signage, audio, data, and displays, the relevant question is not whether each system works. It is whether the property can operate them together without adding another isolated control path.
