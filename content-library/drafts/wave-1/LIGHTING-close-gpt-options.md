### Close options

#### A
When the room flips from everyday programming to an event, your staff should not be chasing a second console to make lighting keep up. They should be able to move the room in sequence, with the lighting look, display source, and audio state handled from the same interface and in the same operational moment.

That gives your managers a cleaner way to run live changes, because the room can be adjusted in context instead of across separate control paths.

For guests, it means the space lands as one environment instead of changing in pieces.

Guests notice when a room holds together. They do not separate lighting from screens from sound. They read the space as one environment, and the experience is stronger when those changes arrive together.

You keep one operating point for the room, and your guests get one coherent experience instead of a handoff between systems.
