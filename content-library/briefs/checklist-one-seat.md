# Wave 2 — Checklist / one-pager: one seat vs a stack of consoles

**Topic:** What they can see and control from one seat, versus walking a stack of consoles.
**Persona:** Prospects comparing how work actually happens on their floor today.
**Source:** Current LUCI only (reset, lighting, revert, volume as capabilities — not procedures). Mike 01: LUCI is not the stack. **Do not name competitors or partners. Do not write that LUCI replaces anything.**
**CTA:** Book a walkthrough. Sit in one seat and run the same list.

**Cover:**
- Two columns: “From one seat” / “From a stack of consoles.” Rows are jobs, not products: change a look, undo a look, match lighting to the room, set an announcement to the right level, see what is on the wall.
- Current LUCI in the “one seat” column as the interface that works with the A/V they already have — absorbs, does not replace.
- No Q-SYS. No brand fight. No invented stats. No New LUCI dump.
- This can be a checklist they tick for their building today, and a one-pager they take from a demo.
- Close: the walkthrough is that list, in one place.

Constraints for every piece
- Readers are new prospects (property A/V, IT, ops leadership). Not a customer LUCI how-to.
- Always write A/V.
- LUCI works with and absorbs the systems already on the floor. It does not replace them. Do not name partners or competitors (no Q-SYS, no brand fight). Never call LUCI a layer.
- New LUCI is beta, property by property. Not a public splash. Do not write “we launched.”
- Do not invent ROI, percentages, or extra stats. Only facts in this brief.
- Never write the word “easy.”
- Institutional voice. Short. LUCI is the means, not the hero speech.
- This is a lead magnet. It has to help before they have LUCI. Close with a walkthrough, not a LUCI procedure.
