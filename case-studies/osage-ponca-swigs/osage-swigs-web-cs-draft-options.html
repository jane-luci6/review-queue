The challenge: align the architecture and the image

Swigs’ LED wall is not flat. A center section sits behind the stage while two wings angle forward toward the audience. Every cabinet edge and LED module had to meet cleanly where one plane changed to the next.

Physical alignment was only the first half of the job. After the 60 cabinets and 480 panels were in place, the team had to map the 3840 × 1296 image across all three surfaces so content would remain continuous from one end of the wall to the other.

What we built

The team moved from cabinet installation to a commissioned display, expanded audio, and LUCI control integration in 25.5 working hours.

All 60 cabinets went up during the first working day. The team then installed the 480 panels, completed the display mapping and programming, and commissioned the wall. Four presets gave the property a full-wall view, two seven-screen layouts, and a featured center image with content continuing on the outer sections.

The audio work added amplification, five stage speakers, two subwoofers, three in-ceiling speakers, and two new zones for the bar and stage. LUCI integrated those zones, the new video sources, and the other endpoints alongside the property’s existing technology.

Scope

  • Installed and aligned 60 LED cabinets across three angled planes
  • Installed 480 LED panels and terminated cabinet power and data
  • Mapped and programmed one 3840 × 1296 continuous canvas
  • Built four operational presets for full-wall, multi-screen, and featured-center use
  • Integrated seven independent video sources
  • Added two audio zones with five stage speakers, two subwoofers, and three in-ceiling speakers
  • Integrated the new audio and video endpoints into LUCI alongside existing property technology

Results: installation to operation in 25.5 hours

The project had been scheduled for four full working days. A four-person onsite team, supported by LUCI’s home office, completed the primary installation and programming during the first two days. By Thursday, the remaining work had been reduced to a three-item punch list.

  • The three physical planes read as one continuous LED canvas.
  • Four presets reshape the wall for a single large image, two seven-screen arrangements, or a featured center image.
  • Seven independent sources support multi-screen television viewing.
  • Two new audio zones support the bar, stage, everyday operation, and live entertainment.
  • Operators manage the new audio and video endpoints through LUCI alongside the property’s existing technology.

The challenge: make one room serve more than one occasion

Swigs was designed as more than another bar. The venue needed to support drinks and everyday casino activity, sports and television viewing, property promotions, and live entertainment.

The centerpiece also had to belong to the room. Instead of placing a flat screen behind the stage, the design follows three architectural planes: a center section at the back and two wings angled toward the audience. The visual experience needed to feel continuous even as the physical wall changed direction.

What we built

One flexible display and audio environment, with distinct modes for the way Swigs changes throughout the day and across events.

One large-format canvas

The entire wall can carry marketing, branding, promotional content, or large-format video across all three planes.

A multi-screen sports view

Two seven-screen layouts and seven independent sources let the property show multiple channels at the same time.

A live-event backdrop

A featured image can take over the center of the wall while the outer sections continue to carry branded or promotional content.

Audio for the bar and stage

Two new zones, five stage speakers, two subwoofers, and three in-ceiling speakers support everyday operation and live entertainment.

Behind those experiences are 60 cabinets, 480 panels, a 3840 × 1296 canvas, four presets, seven video sources, new amplification, and multiple audio endpoints. LUCI brings the new systems into the property’s existing control environment.

Results: one room, multiple experiences

The finished wall feels like part of the architecture rather than a screen attached to it. Its three planes create a more immersive visual field while operating as one continuous display.

  • Between events, the wall can carry property marketing, branding, and promotional content.
  • For sports viewing, seven independent sources turn the wall into a multi-screen environment.
  • For live entertainment, the center can carry the featured image while the outer sections continue branded content.
  • The expanded audio system supports everyday bar operation and the stage.
  • LUCI gives operators centralized control of the new endpoints alongside the property’s existing technology.
  • A four-person onsite team completed the work in 25.5 hours; the original four-day schedule ended with only a three-item Thursday punch list.
