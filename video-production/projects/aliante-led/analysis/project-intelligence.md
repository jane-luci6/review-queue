## Project overview

Aliante Casino + Hotel + Spa (Las Vegas, Nevada, Boyd Gaming) undertook a major sportsbook remodel anchored by a large curved LED wall. LUCI was already live throughout the rest of the property before the sportsbook project began, orchestrating audio, video, and signage across the casino. The sportsbook remodel brought the new room onto the same LUCI platform already operating the property.

**Confirmed facts:**
- Aliante is a long-time LUCI customer (V0026: "They are a long time customer of ours").
- LUCI software was already deployed throughout the property before the sportsbook remodel (V0026: "We have already deployed Lucy software throughout the property"; EJ transcript: "They already have Lucy on it. They have Lucy on site. Lucy is throughout the site here.").
- The sportsbook project was a full remodel that included a new LED wall (V0026: "currently the sportsbook is getting a brand new LED wall installed"; EJ transcript: "this project is to upgrade the sports book").
- The project integrated the sportsbook and sportsbook bar onto LUCI (EJ transcript: "We're just integrating Lucy into the sports book and the sports book bar").

## Project challenges

Technical, physical, scheduling, coordination, operational, or installation challenges supported by evidence.

**Confirmed:**
- Wall flatness: the wall was not flat; imperfections at plywood seams caused unevenness up to a quarter inch, which on a curved wall causes visible misalignment (Jason transcript: "The wall itself has not been flat. There have been a lot of imperfections, like where the seams are for the plywood, there is sometimes an unevenness of up to a quarter inch. So depending on where it's laying, it can offset the wall and it's a curved wall. And so if you have bumps that are sticking proud on the curved wall, it is definitely going to make it funky").
- Curve alignment: lining up frames with the curve so tiles hold and look like one integrated piece; even a sixteenth of an inch offset can cause tiles to not snap in properly, leaving gaps or preventing installation (EJ transcript: "The challenge is lining up the frames. With the curve, so that the tiles hold and look like it's one piece integrated. That's the challenge, because if the tiles are off, even like a sixteenth of an inch, the frames don't snap in properly and they fall out and you'll see gaps or they won't go on at all").
- GC coordination / out-of-sequence work: the general contractor was supposed to finish ahead of LUCI but didn't; the bar and marble were installed before the LED work was done, creating risk of damage (Jason transcript: "During construction, there were some out-of-sequence items like this bar right here in the front. This should not have been installed before we started. They even had the marble put on there. If there was anything that were to fall while we were doing this work, it would fall on this marble and break it").
- Limited lay-down space: not as much room as would be nice, but enough to get by (Jason transcript: "We didn't have as much lay down space as would be nice, but we had just enough to get it down. And there were a lot more crates, so having to deal with those").
- Tile commissioning: achieving completely flat surface with no visible imperfections between tile heights takes about 3 days of installation and commissioning (V0026: "This will take us about three days to install and commission so that they will be completely flat and no visible imperfections between the height of each of the tiles").

**Strongly implied:**
- The team had to self-correct GC work that wasn't finished properly (Jason transcript: "they took them half the day to go find a plumber... I asked them to leave the planer, and any spot that we've been coming across at this point, we just take care of it ourselves").
- Customs/delivery delay: the truck was waiting on customs release before Saturday morning delivery (Jason transcript: "from starting off when the truck arrived, we were waiting on it to be released from customs and then delivery").

**Unknown:**
- Whether the curve alignment issue was fully resolved or if minor imperfections remain (the transcripts describe the approach but not a final sign-off).
- Specific scheduling constraints beyond the day-4 timeline (no detailed schedule in the transcripts).

## Solution / approach

What was done and how.

**Confirmed:**
- The team adjusted each section of the mounting structure to hold the intended curve so every tile sat flush across the full span (written case study: "The team adjusted each section of the mounting structure to hold the intended curve, so every tile sat flush across the full span"; EJ transcript: "we needed to add the frames, pull them out enough to go with the natural curve. So the tiles sit in flush. And we were able to get that done").
- Installation sequence: frames first, then pull cables for power and data, then install tiles (V0026: "Our process will be to install the frames first. We pull our cables through for power and data and then we install our tiles").
- Data jumpers and power: Jason was doing data jumpers and power, about a third done at time of interview (Jason transcript: "I've been doing the data jumpers and the power. And I am probably about a third of the way done with all that").
- Six frames per spot: three going up, three going down; one jumps to the panel right there and goes to the top, another jumps to the panel below and goes down (Jason transcript: "all these were we have six of them at each spot. Three of them are going to go up, and three of them are going to go down").
- LUCI rack: Novastar H15 to drive the main wall, plus H2; set-top boxes drive content; audio lines to line arrays (Jason transcript: "We have an H fifteen here from Novastar to drive the main wall. We have an H two... the set-top boxes that'll be driving content for the wall... these are the audio lines that are going out to the line arrays").
- Adding the wall to LUCI takes less than a couple of minutes (V0026: "to add this wall into the Lucy software, it will only take a couple of minutes, as in less than one minute").
- Operator workflow: open LUCI, go to sportsbook venue, see icon for video wall, choose from six configurations, tap a box/section, select the input (V0026: "you'll go to the venue, the sportsbook venue, and there will be an icon for just a video wall. We have pre-configured several different layouts for this wall"; written case study: "The operator starts by choosing from six wall configurations... Tap a box, select the game, race, or channel that belongs there, and LUCI sends it to that section of the wall").
- Audio is controlled by section: the team can make one event the focus without changing the rest of the room (written case study: "Audio is controlled by section as well, so the team can make one event the focus without changing the rest of the room").
- Phase 2 (bar ticker): starting Tuesday/Wednesday of the next week, same install process (frames → cables → tiles) (V0026: "It'll be the bar area and we're installing a ticker... we'll be starting that Tuesday or Wednesday of next week").

**Strongly implied:**
- The LUCI rack was built "funky" with patching on the back side because it makes more sense (Jason transcript: "We built it very funky because it makes more sense. This is technically the back side... doing all the patching on the back side makes a lot of sense").
