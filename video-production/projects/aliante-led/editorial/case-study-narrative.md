## Core narrative

Aliante Casino + Hotel + Spa, a long-time LUCI customer, undertook a major sportsbook remodel anchored by one of the largest curved LED walls in Las Vegas — 106 feet wide, roughly 2,000 square feet, built from 972 frames and over 5,800 individual tiles. LUCI was already live throughout the rest of the property, orchestrating audio, video, and signage across the casino. The sportsbook remodel brought the new room onto the same LUCI platform already operating everything else.

Behind the wall, the infrastructure story was one of subtraction: 15 full-size AV racks of legacy gear and more than 20 projectors came out; 2 LUCI racks went in. The contrast between the decommissioned equipment and the completed LUCI rack environment is one of the clearest ways to make "complexity made simple" visible — a massive, technically substantial installation that ultimately runs from the same interface as the rest of the property.

The case study moves from the visual spectacle of the finished wall, back through the work and complexity that produced it, into the simplification that LUCI made possible, and out to the broader property LUCI was already orchestrating — closing back on the wall as the payoff.

---

## Audience takeaway

A casino executive, IT, AV, or operations viewer should understand by the end:

1. **LUCI can deliver a major installation.** The 106-foot curved LED wall is real, built, and operational — not a render or a promise.
2. **Behind the spectacle, LUCI simplifies.** Fifteen racks and 20+ projectors became 2 LUCI racks. The complexity behind the wall was consolidated, not added to.
3. **This is one part of a larger environment LUCI orchestrates.** The sportsbook came onto a platform that was already running 266 displays, 89 audio zones, 92 DTV channels, and 98 digital signage endpoints across Aliante. The wall is the latest endpoint, not a new system.
4. **LUCI is the standard.** One interface, one login, one team — for the wall, the ticker, the audio, and everything else.

## Message hierarchy

Ranked in order of importance:

1. **LUCI orchestrates a massive, complex installation simply.** (Primary product meaning — complexity made simple)
2. **The wall is real and substantial.** (Primary visual evidence — scale, installation, finished payoff)
3. **Behind the wall, LUCI subtracted.** (Major visual proof — 15 racks → 2, 20+ projectors removed)
4. **This is bigger than one wall.** (Secondary expansion — property-wide orchestration, already-live partnership)
5. **LUCI executed the installation.** (Supporting credibility — 972 frames, 5,800+ tiles, curve precision, 3-day commissioning)

## Opening strategy

**Recommended: Open on the finished wall, then rewind into the build.**

**Why this is the strongest opening:**

1. **The finished wall footage is the best in the library.** V0036 (7.6s hero pull-back with people for scale) and V0040 (16.3s comprehensive journey through the finished sportsbook) are the most visually compelling assets available. Opening on them gives the viewer immediate spectacle.

2. **It creates a natural question.** The viewer sees the finished wall and wonders: how did this get here? Who built this? What's behind it? That question drives the rewind into the build (Beat 2) and the rack room (Beat 3).

3. **It avoids the "construction video" positioning risk.** If the video opens on construction footage (workers on lifts, scaffolding), the first impression reads as "LUCI builds walls" — a construction company, not an orchestration platform. Opening on the finished wall reads as "this is an impressive installation" — and then the story earns the right to show the work and the simplification behind it.

4. **The rack room contrast lands harder after the wall.** If the viewer has already seen the finished wall (Beat 1), then sees the decommissioned projectors (V0048) and the 2 LUCI racks (V0051), the contrast is more impactful because they know what the simplification produced.

5. **The 5-photo progression has a natural narrative function.** If the story opens on the finished wall and rewinds, the 5-photo progression can serve as the visual mechanism for the rewind — the wall "unbuilds" or the viewer dissolves back through the construction stages. This gives the progression a purpose rather than being a standalone interlude.

**Alternative considered — build toward the reveal:** Opening on construction context (V0021 COMING SOON) and building through installation toward the hero reveal. This is a valid structure, but it asks the viewer to wait 60-90 seconds for the payoff — and the payoff (the finished wall) is the strongest footage. It also risks the "construction video" read in the opening seconds. The build-toward-reveal structure could work if the music and editing are strong enough to sustain tension, but the finished-wall-first structure is lower-risk and higher-impact.

**Decision:** Open on the finished wall (V0036 or V0040). Rewind into the build.

## Ending strategy

**Recommended: Close back on the finished wall as a bookend.**

The video opened on the finished wall (Beat 1). The ending returns to it — but now the viewer understands what's behind it (the build, the rack consolidation, the simplification, the property-wide context). The same image means something different the second time.

**Final visual:** V0036 (hero pull-back) or I0065 (definitive hero still) as the final freeze frame. If the 5-photo progression was used as the rewind in Beat 2, the final image I0027 (completed wall) can serve as the bookend match — the wall "rebuilds" to the same state the viewer saw at the open.

**Conceptual payoff:** The closing message is not "LUCI builds walls" — it is "one wall, one platform, one standard for the whole property." The wall is the visual; the orchestration is the meaning. The ending should land on the wall as the visual payoff of that idea.

## Role of rack consolidation

Rack consolidation is a **major visual proof point of the simplification message** (Layer 2), not peripheral supporting proof.

**How it functions in the narrative:**

Beat 3 (What came out) is where the rack room before/after makes simplification visible. The viewer has already seen the finished wall (Beat 1) and the build (Beat 2). Now they see what was removed behind it:

- **V0048** — decommissioned legacy projectors (dusty, detached lenses, cable spools). This is the "before" of the infrastructure — not the old system operating, but the old system removed.
- **V0051** — completed LUCI racks (three racks with active blue displays). This is the "after" — 2 LUCI racks where 15 full-size AV racks used to be.
- **I0078** — LUCI IPTV ENCODER NX-5 close-up. This is the hardware detail that anchors "this is what runs it."

The contrast between V0048 and V0051 is one of the clearest ways available to make "complexity made simple" visible. The 15-racks-to-2 consolidation makes subtraction concrete and verifiable — the viewer can see the physical footprint reduction, not just hear a claim about it.

**Relationship to the wall story:** The rack consolidation serves the wall story's meaning. The wall is the spectacle; the rack room is where the spectacle's business meaning becomes visible. The video does not become "the rack consolidation video" — the rack room is a beat within the wall story, not a separate thesis.

**Visual quality bridge:** Rack room footage (VQ 2-4) is lower quality than hero wall footage (VQ 4-5). The edit must bridge this gap — likely through music, pacing, and onscreen text (15 → 2) rather than expecting the rack room footage to carry the same visual weight as the hero wall. The contrast is the point, not the polish.

**Onscreen text support:** Property-wide stats (266 displays, 89 audio zones, 92 DTV channels, 98 digital signage endpoints, 7+ technologies) as onscreen text over the B-roll. The numbers do the work; the footage provides the visual context.

## Role of the five-photo progression

**Recommendation: Likely useful — as the rewind mechanism in Beat 2.**

**Where it fits in the narrative:** The 5-photo progression (I0054 → I0059 → I0062 → I0008 → I0027) is the natural visual device for the rewind from the finished wall (Beat 1) into the build (Beat 2). The viewer sees the completed wall, then the wall "unbuilds" or dissolves back through the construction stages: scaffolding → early build → later pre-install → LED installation underway → completed wall.

**Why it works here:**
- It gives the progression a narrative function (the rewind mechanism) rather than being a standalone interlude.
- It bridges the visual quality gap — the progression is photo-based, so it can be treated stylistically (dissolve, flipbook, crop/reframe) without competing with the video footage.
- It pays off at both ends: the opening (Beat 1) shows the finished wall; the progression's final image (I0027, completed wall) matches the opening, creating a bookend.

**Alternative placement (if not used in Beat 2):**
- **End-of-video recap** — the wall "rebuilds" as a bookend before the final payoff (Beat 7). The progression runs forward this time, from scaffolding to completed, landing on the same image the viewer saw at the open.
- **Brief interlude** — a short construction progression within Beat 2, between video installation footage (V0028, V0027) and the rack room (Beat 3).

**Transition/motion treatment:** Not determined here. The candidate treatments (soft dissolve, flipbook, crop/reframe, longer hold on payoff) are recorded in `visual-analysis.json` under `human_curated_sequences` and remain options for the design/edit stage.

**If omitted entirely:** The story still works. The video installation footage (V0028, V0027) can carry the build without the photo progression. The progression is a useful device, not a structural dependency.

## Narrative gaps

Moments where the story requires information or visual proof we do not possess. Prefer restructuring around the gap rather than inventing unsupported explanation.

### Gap 1 — No LUCI interface/iPad footage (largest gap)

**What the story needs:** Beat 4 (Complexity made simple) wants to show HOW LUCI controls the wall — the interface, the iPad, the configuration switching.

**What we have:** No interface footage. No iPad footage. No configuration-switching footage.

**How the narrative restructures around it:** Beat 4 is carried by onscreen text/graphics layered over the visual contrast already established in Beat 3 (rack room before/after) and the finished wall footage (V0036/V0040). The simplification claim is made through the contrast (15 racks → 2) and the breadth (property-wide stats), not through a product demonstration. If Beat 4 cannot land as a standalone beat, it should be shortened and folded into Beat 3 — the rack room contrast IS the simplification message, and the onscreen text (6 configurations, iPad, < 1 minute) can appear over the rack room footage rather than needing its own beat.

### Gap 2 — No customer voice

**What the story needs:** A customer-side operator quote to anchor credibility.

**What we have:** All speakers are LUCI team members. V0024/V0026 are excluded as A-roll.

**How the narrative restructures around it:** The story is told through visuals, onscreen text, and music — not interview audio. This is a production constraint, not a narrative gap. The story works without a customer voice; the visual evidence (the wall, the racks, the property) carries the argument.

### Gap 3 — No true "before" of the old system operating

**What the story needs:** Footage of the old projector/screen sportsbook running, to show what was replaced.

**What we have:** V0048 shows decommissioned projectors in storage — not the old system operating.

**How the narrative restructures around it:** The "before" is implied through the decommissioning footage (V0048 — "this came out") rather than shown as a live former system. The story frames the rack room as "what was removed" (Beat 3), not "what was running before." This avoids implying we can show the old system in operation.

### Gap 4 — No wall-configuration switching footage

**What the story needs:** Beat 4 wants to show the 6 configurations and the tap-a-box workflow.

**What we have:** No footage of configuration switching.

**How the narrative restructures around it:** The configurations are an onscreen text/graphic element in Beat 4, not a visual demonstration. If the beat is folded into Beat 3 (per Gap 1), the configurations appear as text over the rack room or finished wall footage.

### Gap 5 — No footage of the bar ticker under construction (only testing)

**What the story needs:** Beat 5 wants to show the bar ticker being built.

**What we have:** V0031 shows the ticker during testing with test numbers (not finished content). V0039 shows the completed ticker.

**How the narrative restructures around it:** Beat 5 uses V0039 (completed ticker) as the payoff and V0031 as the "during" context — clearly labeled as testing, not finished. The beat is brief (the "also" mention) and does not require construction footage to land.
