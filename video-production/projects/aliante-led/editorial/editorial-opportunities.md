## Direction A — "The Build"

### Core idea
The story follows the physical construction of the 106-foot curved LED wall — from empty room through scaffolding, framing, LED panel installation, to the completed hero reveal. The physical transformation IS the story.

### What it proves
LUCI can execute a major installation quickly and precisely, even on a curved wall that wasn't flat. The wall builds itself on screen.

### Why Aliante is a good example
The wall is one of the biggest in Las Vegas. The curve challenge (quarter-inch imperfections at plywood seams, sixteenth-of-an-inch tile alignment) is project-specific. Day 4 of install shows speed. 972 frames, 5,800+ tiles.

### Visual backbone
- **V0028** (installation progress — workers on scissor lift, green test panels)
- **V0027** (LED wall construction — workers on boom lift and scissor lift)
- **V0031** (bar ticker during testing — test numbers visible)
- **V0035** (commissioning — technicians at base of completed wall)
- **V0036** / **V0040** (hero reveal payoff — full curved wall with live content and patrons)
- **I0065** (definitive hero still)
- **5-photo progression** (I0054 → I0059 → I0062 → I0008 → I0027) — CENTRAL structural device

### Likely structural arc
Establish the space (V0021 COMING SOON) → build begins (V0028, V0027 workers on lifts) → the curve challenge (onscreen text: Jason's wall-flatness quote) → tiles going up → bar ticker testing (V0031) → commissioning (V0035) → payoff: completed hero reveal (V0036, V0040, I0065).

### Strongest supporting claims/facts
- 972 frames, 5,800+ tiles (claim #4, confirmed)
- 106-foot curved LED wall (claim #1, confirmed)
- Day 4 of install at time of filming (claim: install timeline, confirmed)
- Curve alignment to one-sixteenth of an inch (claim #16, confirmed)
- 3 days to install and commission tiles (project intelligence, confirmed)
- "One of the biggest" in Las Vegas (claim #2, qualified)

### Potential use of the 5-photo progress sequence
**CENTRAL.** The progression from scaffolding to completed wall IS the structural backbone. The viewer watches the wall build itself in place. This is the primary use case for the matched-viewpoint sequence.

### Weaknesses or risks
- Doesn't show what LUCI DOES as a platform — no interface footage, no operator workflow. The story is about construction, not orchestration. A viewer might conclude "LUCI builds walls" rather than "LUCI orchestrates." No customer voice. Risks mispositioning LUCI as a construction company.

### Production implications
Music-led with onscreen text for key stats (972 frames, 5,800+ tiles, 106 feet, day 4). The 5-photo progression could be a flipbook or dissolve sequence. No VO (no customer interview, V0024/V0026 excluded).

## Direction B — "Fifteen to Two"

### Core idea
The story centers on what happened behind the wall — the rack room transformation. 15 racks of legacy gear and 20+ projectors came out; 2 LUCI racks went in. The visible wall is the guest-facing result; the invisible rack room is where the consolidation actually happened.

### What it proves
LUCI doesn't just build a wall — it consolidates the infrastructure behind it. The platform replaces hardware, not just displays. Fewer racks, fewer projectors, fewer things to manage.

### Why Aliante is a good example
15 → 2 racks is a concrete, verifiable consolidation. 20+ projectors removed. The former system was rear projection from the early 2000s, running 24/7/365. The footprint reduction is "astounding" (V0026, qualitative). This is the subtraction story LUCI's brand voice demands.

### Visual backbone
- **V0048** (decommissioned projectors — dusty, detached lenses, cable spools)
- **V0053** (decommissioned rack units on floor)
- **V0049** / **V0050** (LUCI hardware being installed — progress)
- **V0051** (completed LUCI racks — the "after," three racks with active blue displays)
- **I0078** (LUCI IPTV ENCODER NX-5 close-up — strongest hardware shot)
- **V0052** (alternate rack room completed — cable management)
- Payoff contrast: V0036 / V0040 (hero wall) + V0039 (bar ticker) showing what the consolidated platform now runs
- B-Roll: **V0004** (food court digital signage), **V0010** (bar with gaming terminals) showing the property the platform operates

### Likely structural arc
Establish the former system (V0048 decommissioned projectors — "this came out") → the removal (V0053) → what went in (V0049, V0050 LUCI hardware) → the result (V0051, I0078 — "two racks") → what it now runs (V0036/V0040 hero wall, V0039 bar ticker, V0004/V0010 B-roll showing the property).

### Strongest supporting claims/facts
- 15 racks → 2 LUCI racks (claim #5, confirmed)
- 20+ projectors removed (claim #6, confirmed)
- Former system: rear projection, state of the art in early 2000s (claim #17, confirmed)
- Property-wide scope: 266 displays, 89 audio zones, 92 DTV channels, 98 digital signage endpoints, 7+ technologies (claim #22, confirmed)
- Cost savings "insurmountable" (claim #19, qualified — qualitative only, NO dollar figure)

### Potential use of the 5-photo progress sequence
**OPTIONAL.** Could be a brief interlude showing the wall going up while the rack room story is the main thread. But the progression doesn't directly support the consolidation argument — it shows the wall, not the racks.

### Weaknesses or risks
Rack room footage is lower visual quality than hero wall footage (VQ 2-4 vs 4-5). The contrast between "dusty projectors" and "clean LUCI racks" is compelling but requires careful editing to make the rack room visually interesting. No interface footage means the story can't show HOW LUCI controls what's in the racks. No customer voice.

### Production implications
Onscreen text for key numbers (15 → 2, 20+ projectors). The old-to-new rack room contrast could be a visual match-cut. Music-led. No VO.

## Direction C — "One Platform"

### Core idea
The story establishes that LUCI was already running the entire property — 266 displays, 89 audio zones, 92 DTV channels, 98 digital signage endpoints — and then the sportsbook remodel brought the new wall onto the same platform. The sportsbook isn't a new system; it's the latest endpoint on an existing one.

### What it proves
LUCI is a property-wide orchestration engine, not a sportsbook install. The new wall is impressive, but the deeper story is that it runs from the same interface as everything else. One login, one standard, one team.

### Why Aliante is a good example
Aliante is a long-time LUCI customer. LUCI was already live throughout the property before the sportsbook project began. The sportsbook was a natural extension, not a new sale. The property-wide scope is verifiable from the written case study ledger. This is the "platform grows without per-seat licensing / parallel systems" beat.

### Visual backbone
- B-Roll property tour: **V0002** (casino floor), **V0004** (food court with digital signage), **V0010** (bar with gaming terminals), **V0014** / **V0016** (hotel lobby) — establishes the property
- **V0021** (COMING SOON sportsbook branding — the new addition arrives)
- **V0028** (installation — the wall goes up)
- Sportsbook hero: **V0036** / **V0040** / **I0065** (the payoff — the latest endpoint)
- Bar ticker: **V0039** (completed), **V0031** (during testing)
- Behind the wall: **V0051** / **I0078** (LUCI racks), **V0048** (what came out)
- The breadth of environments shown (casino floor, food court, bar, lobby, sportsbook, rack room) IS the visual argument for "one platform"

### Likely structural arc
Establish the property (B-Roll tour) → the sportsbook arrives (V0021 COMING SOON, V0028 installation) → the wall goes live (V0036, V0040 hero) → the bar ticker (V0039, V0031) → behind the wall (V0051, I0078, V0048) → the scope: one property, one platform (B-Roll breadth + property-wide stats via onscreen text).

### Strongest supporting claims/facts
- LUCI already live throughout the property (claim #9, confirmed)
- Long-time LUCI customer (claim #10, confirmed)
- Property-wide: 266 displays, 89 audio zones, 92 DTV channels, 98 digital signage endpoints, 7+ technologies (claim #22, confirmed)
- Adding the wall to LUCI took less than a couple of minutes (claim #14, confirmed)
- One interface, one login (written case study, confirmed)
- 15 racks → 2 LUCI racks (claim #5, confirmed)

### Potential use of the 5-photo progress sequence
**OPTIONAL.** Could work as a brief interlude (the wall building itself) within the broader property story. But the property-wide story doesn't depend on it — the breadth of B-Roll is the argument.

### Weaknesses or risks
No interface footage means the "one platform" claim must be made through onscreen text and the breadth of environments shown, not through a demonstration of the interface. The B-Roll is good but not spectacular — it's property context, not hero footage. The story risks feeling like a property tour rather than a case study with a clear thesis. No customer voice.

### Production implications
Onscreen text for property-wide stats (266 displays, 89 zones, etc.). Music-led. The B-Roll tour + sportsbook hero + rack room could work as a three-act structure (property → sportsbook → behind-the-scenes). No VO.

## Direction D — "The Curve"

### Core idea
The story focuses on the technical challenge and craft of installing LED on a curved wall that wasn't flat. The wall had quarter-inch imperfections at plywood seams; even a sixteenth-of-an-inch offset causes tiles to not snap in. The team adjusted each section to hold the intended curve so every tile sat flush. This is a story about precision, expertise, and the difference between "putting up a wall" and "putting up a curved wall that looks like one piece."

### What it proves
LUCI's installation expertise is not generic — it's project-specific. The curve challenge is real, documented, and overcome. The payoff is a wall that looks seamless because someone solved the geometry.

### Why Aliante is a good example
The curve challenge is unique to this project. Jason and EJ both describe it in detail in the transcripts. The precision (sixteenth of an inch) is concrete and credible. The wall's curvature is visible in the hero footage.

### Visual backbone
- **V0028** (installation with workers on lifts)
- **V0027** (LED modules going up against green test pattern)
- **V0035** (commissioning with technicians at base)
- **V0036** / **V0040** (hero reveal showing the seamless curve)
- **I0065** (hero still showing the curve)
- **5-photo progression** (I0054 → I0059 → I0062 → I0008 → I0027) — USEFUL, shows the wall taking shape

### Likely structural arc
Establish the challenge (V0021 COMING SOON, then V0028 construction begins) → the problem (onscreen text: "the wall was not flat... quarter-inch imperfections... on a curved wall") → the work (V0027, V0028 workers on lifts, tiles going up) → the precision (V0035 commissioning) → the payoff: the seamless curve (V0036, V0040, I0065 — the wall looks like one piece).

### Strongest supporting claims/facts
- 106-foot curved LED wall (claim #1, confirmed)
- 972 frames, 5,800+ tiles (claim #4, confirmed)
- Curve alignment to one-sixteenth of an inch (claim #16, confirmed)
- 3 days to install and commission (project intelligence, confirmed)
- "One of the biggest" in Las Vegas (claim #2, qualified)
- Wall flatness challenge: quarter-inch imperfections at plywood seams (project intelligence, confirmed)

### Potential use of the 5-photo progress sequence
**USEFUL.** The progression shows the wall taking shape, and the curve challenge is visible in the transition from framing to panels. But it's supporting, not central — the curve story is about precision, not just about time-lapse.

### Weaknesses or risks
The story is about installation craft, not about what LUCI does as a platform. A viewer might admire the craftsmanship without understanding why LUCI matters. The technical challenge requires explanation (onscreen text) since there's no VO. No customer voice. The rack room and bar ticker are secondary to this direction.

### Production implications
Onscreen text for the precision quotes (Jason's wall-flatness description, EJ's sixteenth-of-an-inch quote). Music-led, possibly with a build-and-reveal structure. The 5-photo progression could be a brief interlude. No VO.

### Major tradeoffs

**A vs B:** Direction A has the strongest visual evidence (hero reveals, 5-photo progression) but the weakest positioning fit — it tells a construction story, not a platform story. Direction B has the strongest factual evidence and positioning fit (the 15 → 2 consolidation IS LUCI's brand voice) but weaker visual evidence (rack room footage is lower quality).

**C vs B:** Direction C has the strongest property-wide facts but risks feeling like a property tour. Direction B is more focused — it has a clear thesis (subtraction/consolidation) and a clear visual contrast (dusty projectors → clean LUCI racks). C is broader but less sharp.

**D vs A:** Both are installation-led. D is more specific (the curve challenge) but narrower — it doesn't show the rack room, bar ticker, or property context. A is broader but less distinctive. Both have weak positioning fit.

**The strongest direction combines B's thesis with A's visual payoff:** the consolidation story (15 → 2) as the argument, the hero wall reveal as the visual payoff, and the 5-photo progression as a supporting device within that structure.

## Recommendation

**recommended_direction:** B — "Fifteen to Two"

The consolidation story is the LUCI differentiator. No competitor tells this story. The 15 → 2 rack transformation is concrete, verifiable, and project-specific. It aligns perfectly with LUCI's established brand voice: subtraction over addition, fewer things to manage, "the difference in the sheer amount of footprint is astounding." The visual contrast between dusty decommissioned projectors (V0048) and clean blue-lit LUCI racks (V0051, I0078) is a compelling before/after that doesn't require a customer voice to land. The hero wall footage (V0036, V0040, I0065) serves as the guest-facing payoff — "this is what the consolidated platform now runs." The 5-photo progression can optionally appear as a brief interlude showing the wall building itself, but the story doesn't depend on it.

**strongest_alternative:** A — "The Build"

If the Strategic Marketer prioritizes visual spectacle over brand positioning, Direction A has the strongest visual evidence. The 5-photo matched-viewpoint progression is a unique editorial device that no other LUCI case study can replicate. The hero reveals (V0036, V0040) are the strongest footage in the library. The risk is that the story tells "LUCI builds walls" rather than "LUCI orchestrates platforms" — but this could be mitigated by framing the build as evidence of execution capability within a consolidation story (effectively merging A into B).

**Note for Strategic Marketer:** These are not the only options. A hybrid approach — B's thesis + A's visual payoff + the 5-photo progression as interlude — may be stronger than any single direction. The decision belongs to the next stage.
