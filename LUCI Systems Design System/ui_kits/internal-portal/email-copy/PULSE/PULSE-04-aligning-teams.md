# PULSE-04 — Aligning teams

- **Audience:** Warm and net-new prospects eligible for long-term nurture
- **Cycle:** Every 4–6 weeks
- **Timing:** Month 4
- **Purpose:** Tell the story of an incident or decision crossing teams with no single owner. Tie in-product context, audit history, and the embedded team to one accountable response. Accountability is part of the alignment story, not a separate capability.
- **CTA:** See how LUCI aligns the operation by default.
- **Status:** draft v1 for Jane review

## Strategy

### What we’re writing and why
The fourth Pulse email tells one incident story to show alignment and accountability as the same operating behavior. It avoids presenting audit history, in-product context, and the embedded team as unrelated features.

### For whom
Operations and IT leaders who have seen a visible issue bounce among teams because nobody holds both the operating context and the response path.

### Best way to reach them
Send from LUCI Marketing in Month 4. Use a generic pre-event screen incident, follow the context from report to response, and link to a workflow proof.

### How to make impact while staying professional
Make ownership tangible: the history shows what changed, the request carries the relevant context, and the embedded team stays with the issue.

### Primary message
When an A/V incident crosses teams, connected context, audit history, and an embedded team create one accountable response.

### Direction 3 — Who owns the whole answer?

Operations can describe the impact. IT can inspect the device. Marketing can identify the intended content. But who owns the whole answer when the incident crosses all three?

LUCI keeps the operating context together. Audit history records who did what and when. A support request carries device, incident, and error context into the response. The embedded LUCI team stays engaged with the property instead of handing off a disconnected ticket.

## Why the final won
**Direction 1 — The screen before doors open**

It gives the month-four message urgency without dramatizing the outcome, and it lets each team contribute a legitimate part of the truth. The final takes Direction 2’s connected-work language and Direction 3’s whole-answer frame.
