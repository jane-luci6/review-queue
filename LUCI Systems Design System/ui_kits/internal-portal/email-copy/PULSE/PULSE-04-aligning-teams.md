# PULSE-04 — One accountable incident response

- **Audience:** Warm and net-new prospects eligible for long-term nurture
- **Cycle:** Every 4–6 weeks
- **Timing:** Month 4
- **Purpose:** Show how audit history, incident context, and the LUCI team keep one visible A/V issue connected from report through response.
- **CTA:** See how LUCI aligns the operation by default.
- **Status:** draft r2 for Jane review

## Strategy

### What we’re writing and why
Tell one pre-event LED wall incident from first report through diagnosis. Treat alignment and accountability as one response path.

### For whom
Operations and IT leaders who have watched visible issues lose context as they cross teams.

### Best way to reach them
Send from LUCI Marketing in Month 4. Use the wall incident, name the evidence attached to it, and link to one workflow proof.

### How to make impact while staying professional
The dark-wall line adds a human truth; audit history, logs, monitoring, and diagnosis make ownership concrete.

### Primary message
Audit history, incident context, and LUCI's monitoring team keep one visible A/V issue connected from report through response.

## Three draft directions

### Direction 1 — Twenty minutes before doors
Dear #Name#,

An LED wall goes dark 20 minutes before doors open. The floor calls operations, operations calls IT, and someone asks who changed what. A dark wall is very good at finding an audience.

LUCI connects the property's A/V, audit history, and incident context. A request carries device details and logs; the LUCI team sees the same evidence, diagnoses the issue, and supports the response.

The incident keeps its context while the people keep their roles.

### Direction 2 — Stop forwarding the problem
Dear #Name#,

The floor sends a photo. Operations forwards it to IT. IT asks what changed. The wall remains dark while the message gains recipients.

LUCI orchestrates the property's A/V and keeps the incident record attached. Audit history shows who did what and when. Device details and logs travel with the request, and the LUCI team monitors the environment, diagnoses the issue, and supports the response.

More names on the thread do not create ownership. Shared evidence does.

See the workflow: [PROOF URL]

### Direction 3 — Who changed what?
Dear #Name#,

When a wall stops showing what it should, the first useful question is often, “Who changed what?” The least useful answer is a tour of three systems.

LUCI runs the property's A/V with audit history and incident context in one place. Teams can see the action, person, and time. A request includes relevant device details and logs, so the LUCI team can diagnose and support the response from the same record.

## Why the final won
**Direction 1 — Twenty minutes before doors**

It creates urgency without inventing an outcome and gives each handoff a concrete reason. The final borrows Direction 2's attached record and Direction 3's direct diagnosis language.
