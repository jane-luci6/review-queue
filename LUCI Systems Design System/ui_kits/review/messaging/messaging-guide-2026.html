Tagline and value proposition

The Orchestration Engine for Enterprise Multimedia
One interface to control, automate, and execute the entire guest experience.

Value proposition

Separate A/V, signage, building, and operating systems create a longer list of vendors, interfaces, and handoffs. LUCI brings that technology into one operating platform and gives the property team one interface to control, automate, and execute the guest experience, backed by an embedded team that stays. The result is fewer variables, less dedicated hardware, and one accountable operation that can extend as the property changes.

The LUCI worldview

Complex A/V environments rarely fail because they lack another feature. They fail because each renovation, vendor, processor, control interface, and workaround adds another decision the property must carry.

LUCI applies subtraction. One operating interface consolidates duplicate control paths. A common standard reduces the vendors, logins, rack equipment, and workflows that operations and IT must coordinate.

The software is only half of the answer. The embedded team learns the property, carries design intent into deployment, supports the live environment, and returns field experience to the platform. The client gets a shorter list of things to manage and a team that stays.

The offering

LUCI Systems is one offering: the orchestration platform and the embedded partnership that plans, deploys, supports, and refines it. Never sell the software as if the team were an add-on, and never describe the team as a separate consulting engagement.

  • The platform

    Software that connects the property’s A/V, signage, building, and content systems through one interface on standard network infrastructure.

  • The partnership

    The LUCI FDE and embedded team carry context from early planning through deployment, training, support, and continuous refinement.

  • The hardware

    A preconfigured LUCI appliance supports the platform. Do not turn the appliance into the product story.

  • Where people work

Message pillars (brand, permanent)

These four pillars anchor the brand. The three New LUCI pillars organize the beta story and belong in that time-bound section.

  • 01Complete visibility and control across the property

    Bring displays, LED walls, audio zones, signage, lighting, sources, and their current state into one operating view. Teams see the same property and act from the same interface.

  • 02Align teams by default

    Operations, IT, Marketing, A/V, and leadership work from shared schedules, device state, permissions, and incident context. The system keeps the evidence connected while each team keeps its role.

  • 03Fully activate the guest experience

    Coordinate screens, sources, sound, signage, and lighting around the moment the property needs to deliver—from a scheduled morning reset to a live event change.

  • 04Invest in A/V that scales and improves

    Extend one operating standard into added rooms, venues, and properties. Continuous refinement adds capability without forcing the organization to rebuild its operating model around each project.

  • Design & Deployment

  • Embedded Partnership

Key features (platform today)

Use these capabilities when describing the platform clients run now. Keep beta capabilities in the New LUCI section.

    A live property map gives authorized users one place to find and operate displays, LED walls, audio zones, signage, lighting, and connected endpoints.

  • Open integration

    LUCI connects to A/V, signage, building, and content systems through open protocols and APIs while preserving the property’s hardware choices.

  • Presets & scheduling

What LUCI is and isn’t

LUCI IS

  • The orchestration engine for enterprise multimedia
  • One offering made of software, a LUCI appliance, an operating standard, and an embedded team
  • A platform that works with the A/V and operational technology a property already runs
  • One interface for control, automation, execution, and shared operating context
  • A continuing relationship from planning through support and refinement

LUCI IS NOT

  • An orchestration layer or middleware category
  • A room-by-room control panel
  • A reporting dashboard that stops at visibility
  • A point solution that creates another integration problem
  • A wholesale technology swap or a substitute for the client’s A/V team
  • A vendor that hands the property off after deployment

When a physical wall, projector, rack, or headend is literally removed and another item takes its place, say so. When the property keeps its technology and LUCI brings it into one operating standard, use “works with,” “connects,” “absorbs,” “integrates,” “consolidates,” or “orchestrates.”

Voice and tone

A property leader may meet LUCI through a search result, a forwarded deck, or one email with no prior context. Give that reader the operating problem first, define LUCI in one plain line, and name the room, screen, source, user, or decision that makes the problem real.

The rules in practice

  • Lead with accumulation, then subtraction.

    Name the separate controls, vendors, rack equipment, and handoffs the property carries. Then show the shorter operating list LUCI creates.

  • Use active voice and specific nouns.

    “The banquet manager stages the ballroom screens and audio” tells the reader who acts, where, and on what. “The experience is optimized” tells them nothing.

  • Describe institutions, not feelings.

    State what the platform, team, access model, or operating standard does. Do not rely on adjectives about how the software feels.

  • Write lean copy with rhythm.

    Cut modifiers that prop up weak claims. Keep a short sentence when it lands; use a longer sentence when sequence or cause matters.

  • Keep the stakes operational.

    The room opens, the wall changes, the incident keeps its context, and the team remains accountable. Avoid abstract aspiration.

  • Use “replace” only for a literal substitution.

    The house rule permits literal use; the prospect email campaign bans the word in every form. needs Jane's call

Say this / not this

SAY THIS

  • LUCI runs the property’s A/V from one interface.
  • The bartender changes the bar screens without opening controls for the rest of the floor.
  • The request reaches the LUCI team with device context and logs attached.
  • The new room joins the operating standard already in place.
  • The embedded team stays through planning, commissioning, support, and refinement.

NOT THIS

  • LUCI is an easy, intuitive orchestration layer.
  • We transform and empower the guest experience.
  • LUCI replaces your current technology.
  • Best-in-class control for every property.
  • In a casino, operational precision is everything.

Register by medium

  • Website

    Start with a recognizable property problem, define LUCI quickly, and support the claim with a workflow, scope count, or operating result. Second person is allowed.

  • Executive / proposal

    Frame accumulation, risk, ownership, standardization, and the continuing operating model. Keep the argument factual and useful to the full buying group.

  • Email

    One idea, one scene, one action. Define LUCI for a cold reader when list memory cannot be assumed. Keep prospect emails under 150 words.

  • Social

    Use a wall, rack, room, or before-and-after as the hook; close on one interface, one operating view, or one accountable team.

  • Video

    Let the property scene and action carry the explanation. Use conversational delivery without slang or unsupported superlatives.

  • SOW

    Use third person, specific scope, factual obligations, and no marketing register.

Retired words and patterns

Retire absurdly simple, easy, intuitive, revolutionize, transform, empower, best-in-class, game-changing, owner’s rep, percentage claims, unnamed superlatives, repeated “In a casino…” openings, and any use of layer that names LUCI.
