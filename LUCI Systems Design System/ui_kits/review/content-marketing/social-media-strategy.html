Why we’re posting

LUCI’s most visible artifact — the LED wall, the rack — is also its least important message. The thing that actually matters is abstract: one interface to control, automate, and execute the entire guest experience. Social is where we practice showing the visible thing without letting it become the headline. Done well, each post is a small, repeatable demonstration that the gear is evidence of a shorter list of things to manage — not the product.

The business goal is narrow and honest: stay present in the feed of the small group of people who would call LUCI — IT and operations leadership at gaming and hospitality properties — so that when a project lands on their desk, LUCI is already familiar. We are not chasing reach. We are chasing recognizability with about forty people.

What this strategy is not. It is not a growth-hacking plan, not a follower-count play, and not a place to announce un-named clients or un-launched integrations. It is a discipline for posting consistently, on-brand, behind a sign-off gate, until Mike trusts the cadence enough to loosen it.

Who we’re talking to

LinkedIn for this category is not an A/V or facilities audience. The people who scroll past our posts — and the people we want to — sit one or two levels above the gear. They are IT leadership and operations leadership, with the rest of the buyer committee (CEO/GM, CFO, CMO) in supporting orbit. The A/V and facilities manager is an early assessor in a deal, rarely the person we’re trying to make call us from a post.

That changes the copy. We write for the person running the property, not the person racking the gear. A post that lands for a CIO sounds like this:

“One interface for every system on the property — on standard network infrastructure, without proprietary hardware or specialist programming.”

— CIO / IT Director line, Messaging Guide

And for the operations leader who feels floor friction directly:

“See everything. Control anything. From any device. Without leaving the floor.”

— A/V & Facilities Manager line, Messaging Guide (still useful as a hook, even though this person isn’t our primary scroller)

The practical rule: every caption should answer “what does this mean for the person responsible for the property?” — not “what gear is in the frame?” If a caption only makes sense to an A/V technician, it’s written for the wrong reader.

The messaging discipline

This is the single most important rule on this page, because it’s the one the visuals will fight. The LED walls, the racks, the before/after photos, the process video — all of it is proof, never the headline. We worked this out once for the consolidation ledger: a graphic that made the rack reduction look like the headline was wrong, because the brand leads with one interface everywhere and treats hardware reduction as the evidence underneath it. The same discipline applies to every post.

The hook-and-close pattern. The visual is the hook (the wall, the rack, the crew, the before/after). The caption is the close, and the close always lands on the abstraction — one interface, one team, one accountable system. A post that shows a rack being cleaned out and stops at “look how tidy” has failed; the same post that closes with “one rack, one interface, one team accountable for all of it” has done its job.

Diagrams and visuals as the anchor. Because the primary message is abstract, diagrams and structured visuals are not decoration — they are how we keep the audience on the primary message even when the secondary benefit (the visible hardware) is what the camera can actually show. Prefer a diagram-led post over a gear photo whenever the goal is the abstraction. The canonical diagrams (What LUCI is, Consolidation ledger, System architecture, Embedded Operation orbit) are all available and on-brand.

Four pillars — every post routes to one. From the Messaging Guide: 01 Complete visibility and control (one interface). 02 Align your teams by default. 03 Fully activate the guest experience. 04 Invest in the only A/V that scales and improves. If a draft doesn’t clearly serve one of these, it isn’t a LUCI post — cut it or reframe it.

Cadence & weekly rhythm

2–3 posts per week, LinkedIn company page only. Consistency beats volume: a steady two posts a week for six months is worth more than a burst of ten and then silence. The cadence is the brand signal — it says “this company is awake and shipping,” quietly, every week.

The five post types

Every post is one of these five. Each has its own rules, its own pillar, and its own sign-off sensitivity. The mix ratio is a guideline, not a contract — let what’s happening in the field drive the actual split.

1 · Client in-the-field~1 / wk
Pillar 01 or 02 — visibility, teams aligned
  • Nice photography only — no phone-grab stills, no cluttered backgrounds.
  • No client names. No property-identifiable signage, uniforms, or landmarks.
  • State clearly it is a work in progress, not a finished project.
  • Promise the finalized view and keep a placeholder for the reveal post.
  • Close the caption on one interface / one team — not on the gear.
  • No timelines, no scope, no pricing, no “go-live” claims.
Mike sensitivity: highest. Always in the weekly batch; never posted without his sign-off.
2 · Case study & snippet~1 / wk
Pillar 03 or 04 — guest experience, scales & improves
  • Full case study post when one ships (Ameristar, Tachi) — named, with link.
  • Snippets the rest of the time: a single result, quote, or before/after.
  • Video of client sites is encouraged — rack cleanup, before/after, process.
  • Unnamed video is fine; named video only with the client’s OK on file.
  • Heavy-hitting messaging on top of the visual; close on the abstraction.
  • No % boasts or unverified metrics in brand copy (proof rules).
Mike sensitivity: high when named or video-led. Snippets of already-approved case studies ride the batch.
3 · Thought leadership (Mike)~1 / 2 wks
Any pillar — routed by the topic
  • Pull Mike’s real voice: “A Minute with Mike” column, transcripts, quotes.
  • Do not invent a Mike opinion. If we don’t have it on record, we don’t post it.
  • No headshot, no caricature — use a topic diagram (the Recall Model pattern).
  • Phase 1: posted from the company page. Phase 2 (later): Mike’s personal page.
  • Goal: make Mike forward-facing for LUCI over time, not overnight.
  • No ghostwritten first-person that Mike hasn’t read and approved.
Mike sensitivity: absolute. First-person Mike copy is never posted without his direct sign-off.
4 · Industry / partner newslow priority
Pillar 01 — visibility & control, reframed
  • Commentary framing, not pure re-share. Add a LUCI point of view.
  • Never promote a competitor. When in doubt, don’t post.
  • Partner news OK if the partner is a real LUCI integration (and is public).
  • Keep it rare — this is the lowest-priority type and the easiest to skip.
  • No “AV” — always A/V. No naming LUCI a “layer.”
Mike sensitivity: medium. Competitor-risk is the real gate; Jane screens before the batch.
5 · Weekly series1 / wk (Mon)
Pillar 01 or 02 — by series (see below)
  • Recognizable, repeatable format — the cadence is the brand signal.
  • Genuinely useful to casino leadership, not a thinly-veiled product pitch.
  • Same visual template each week so it’s scannable in-feed.
  • Format locked after the transcript-mining pass (see “The weekly series”).
  • No rotating formats week to week — that defeats the recognizability.
Mike sensitivity: low once format is approved. The format approval is the gate; individual weeks ride the batch.

The weekly series

The weekly series is the one post we ship every Monday, same format, so a casino leadership reader starts to recognize it in their feed. Two candidate directions are on the table; we pick one after the transcript-mining pass surfaces what Mike actually has to say. The format stays fixed for at least a quarter once chosen — recognizability only works if we resist re-inventing it.

Candidate A — Property Playbook

A weekly practical tip or short checklist for casino operations leadership — lighter on brand tie-in, heavier on standalone usefulness. The bet: a Dir/VP saves it, sends it to their floor team, and LUCI becomes the brand that handed them a useful thing on a Monday. Pillar 01 (visibility & control) by way of operational clarity. Risk: can drift toward generic ops content if we’re not careful to keep it A/V- and floor-adjacent.

Candidate B — The Shorter List

A weekly post built on LUCI’s variable-reduction principle — one short, sharp illustration of a property reducing the number of things to manage (racks, interfaces, vendors, handoffs, screens-with-no-plan). Each week a different variable, each week a small visual (a before/after list, a consolidation diagram, a one-line reframe). The bet: it trains the audience on the abstraction that is actually LUCI’s product, week after week, without ever sounding like a pitch. Pillar 01 and 02 directly. Risk: needs a steady supply of real examples, which is exactly what the transcript miner is for.

Decision pending. Run node scripts/mine-transcripts.mjs against the transcript history, review what Mike actually has on record, and pick A, B, or a hybrid (e.g. Property Playbook framed around one reduced variable per week). The weekly-series template in the Social Studio is built to accept either format once locked.

Sign-off workflow (weekly batch)

The point of the batch is that Mike does not monitor the feed. He reviews one page, once a week, and the rest of the time he doesn’t think about social. Nothing goes out without his OK; he never has to catch a post he dislikes after the fact. This is the trade that earns the cadence.

  1. 01
    Jane & Cursor · Mon–Tue

    Draft the week’s 2–3 posts in the Social Studio using the per-type templates. Pull from the Content Lab backlog and mined transcripts. Each post gets the hook-and-close check and a pillar tag.

  2. 02
    Jane · Tue

    Self-review pass: anonymization check on field posts, competitor screen on news posts, voice-flag check (A/V not AV, no “layer” for LUCI). Fix or cut before Mike sees it.

  3. 03
    Jane · Tue

    Finalize the week’s posts into social-this-week.html and rebuild the review portal. The page is already linked from the existing Review Queue — no new queue section, just a link.

  4. 04
    Mike · Wed

    Opens the one page in the Internal Marketing Portal, sees the week’s 2–3 drafts with copy and visuals, comments or approves the batch. One sitting, not all week.

  5. 05
    Jane · Wed–Thu

    Apply Mike’s comments. If he kills a post, it’s killed — no revival. If he edits, his edit wins. Re-deploy the page if copy changed materially.

  6. 06
    Jane · Thu–Fri

    Post the approved batch to LinkedIn manually (no API connected today). Mark each post Live in the Content Lab backlog. Archive the week’s page.

Loosening the gate later. Once Mike has approved ~8–10 clean batches, we revisit. The likely first loosening: the weekly series and already-approved-format snippets stop needing per-post sign-off and ride a lighter “heads-up” batch. Client-in-field and first-person Mike copy stay gated indefinitely. Mike decides when, not us.

Industry & partner news guardrails

This is the lowest-priority post type and the one with the most ways to go wrong. The risk isn’t that we post too little news — it’s that we accidentally amplify a competitor or signal an integration that isn’t public. A few hard lines:

  • Commentary, not re-share. A bare re-share of someone else’s announcement is low-value and makes our feed a mirror. If we post it, we add a LUCI point of view on top — one or two sentences on what it means for a property running the floor.

  • No competitor promotion. If a post would put a competitor’s product or name in front of our audience in a positive light, it doesn’t ship. When in doubt, don’t post — the downside is asymmetric.

  • Partner news only when public. A real LUCI integration (e.g. Q-SYS) is fair game for commentary only if the integration is already public. If it’s pre-announcement, the post waits — no exceptions, even indirectly.

  • Honesty Protocol applies. If we credit a partner or backend, we credit them by name. We never imply LUCI owns native zone logic or routing that belongs to a third party. This is a hard brand guardrail, not a social preference.

Voice & terminology

The canonical voice lives in the Messaging Guide and the Personas, and the standing rule is in .cursor/rules/luci-messaging-voice.mdc. The short version for social drafting:

  • Always A/V — never “AV” or “A-V” — in body, captions, alt text, hashtags, and on the visuals themselves.

  • Never “layer” as a noun for LUCI. Use orchestration engine for positioning and LUCI orchestrates… for headlines and capability copy.

  • Describe what the platform does, not how it feels. “Operate every endpoint from one interface” passes; “absurdly simple” does not.

  • Watch repetition. If engine or orchestrates appears more than once in a short caption, rotate to a precise alternative (runs, operates, integrates, consolidates, refines, platform, infrastructure).

  • Institutions, not adjectives. Declarative tone, standardization without apology, subtraction framing.

Anything that drifts from these gets caught in Jane’s self-review pass (step 02 of the sign-off flow) before it reaches Mike.

Where the work happens

Three places, each with a job:

  • Content Lab (ui_kits/content-marketing/index.html) — the input side. Raw sources, mined ideas, voice flags. The transcript miner drops candidate insights here.

  • Social Studio (ui_kits/content-marketing/social-studio.html) — the drafting workspace. One reusable template per post type, so every post is written the same way every week. This is where Jane and Cursor draft the batch.

  • This week’s posts (ui_kits/content-marketing/social-this-week.html) — the clean, Mike-facing review page. Linked from the existing Review Queue in the Internal Marketing Portal; no separate queue section. Rebuilt each week with that week’s finalized drafts.

All three are wired into the Design Hub as a single “Social media” nav space. The standing rule (.cursor/rules/luci-social-media.mdc) makes the agent apply this strategy automatically in future sessions without re-explaining it.
