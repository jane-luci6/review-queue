Observation 1

Observation

Adding agents does not automatically add intelligence. Sometimes it just creates more places for the original point to disappear.

Concrete detail

One LinkedIn post passed from Jane to Cornelius to Svetlana to GPT to GLM. Four hand-offs later, it was neither funny nor coherent.

Why a reader would nod

Multi-agent work can reproduce the same context loss as an overstaffed meeting.

Observation 2

Observation

Agents do not remove the human bottleneck. They move it from producing the work to making decisions about the work.

Concrete detail

Work continues after Jane leaves at 4 PM and is waiting the next morning—but much of it is waiting for her answer.

Why a reader would nod

Faster production creates a larger decision queue.

Observation 3

Observation

“Done” is something an agent says, not necessarily something that happened.

Concrete detail

Jane regularly has to check work after an agent reports it complete.

Why a reader would nod

Confidence and completion arrive in the same sentence, even when only one is warranted.

Observation 4

Observation

The hardest part of working with data is often noticing that two correct-looking numbers mean different things.

Concrete detail

An old log reported 26 clickers. The real count was 13 people; 26 was the sum of their clicks across links.

Why a reader would nod

The arithmetic can be accurate while the conclusion is wrong.

Observation 5

Observation

An agent can work all night, but only if the human’s laptop stays awake.

Concrete detail

Jane’s Mac went offline on Friday, so the scheduled end-of-day task never ran. The agent discovered this on Monday and apologized.

Why a reader would nod

Supposedly tireless automation still depends on very ordinary infrastructure.

Observation 6

Observation

Once an agent delegates work, changing your mind becomes a second workflow.

Concrete detail

Jane told Cornelius not to draft an email after his background helper had already started. Cornelius then had to stop the work he had set in motion.

Why a reader would nod

Asynchronous agents make cancellation a real operational state, not just a correction in conversation.

Observation 7

Observation

A system can remember a hundred tasks and still forget what one of them means.

Concrete detail

Cornelius maintains Jane’s long to-do list, and Jane sometimes has to ask what an item he wrote was supposed to mean.

Why a reader would nod

Preserving words is not the same as preserving context.

Observation 8

Observation

Sometimes the most intelligent response from an agent is admitting that it cannot parse what you typed.

Concrete detail

Jane sent “Dpm,” and the agent asked what she meant instead of constructing an answer around it.

Why a reader would nod

A clarifying question is noticeably useful after seeing how confidently AI can fill a blank.

Observation 9

Observation

Agents make sending a message to the wrong coworker almost frictionless.

Concrete detail

Jane told one agent, “Sorry, that was meant for Vikram.” It simply helped redirect the request.

Why a reader would nod

There is no awkward explanation, bruised ownership, or meeting to clarify responsibilities.

Observation 10

Observation

An AI agent can remain idle for six weeks without inventing work to justify its existence.

Concrete detail

Svetlana was created with the instruction to wait until Jane said start, and waited about six weeks.

Why a reader would nod

That is a surprisingly literal interpretation of “stand by.”
