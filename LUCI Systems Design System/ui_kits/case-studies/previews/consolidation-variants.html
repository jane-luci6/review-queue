Consolidation section — before/after variants

Six directions for making the “before” rack read clearly as the problem, not the result. All keep imagery in the background and reuse the same four before→after rows. The “after” image here is a stand-in (the install head-end photo); the real “after” can be a clean LUCI rack, a finished venue, or an interface screenshot — flagged below each variant where it matters.

Scroll down to compare A–F. Each is a full-width section at realistic size. Pick a direction (or ask for iteration) and I’ll implement it in the canonical yaamava.html, the review mirror, and the website Astro component — then propagate the pattern to Ameristar and any other case study using the rack-background treatment.

Variant A

Vertical split, labeled halves

Background split down the middle — before rack on the left (darkened, desaturated), after on the right (brighter). A 2px mint seam marks the transformation. Text rides a navy scrim over the seam so both halves read. “Before” / “After” tags in the corners remove the ambiguity. Best when you have a strong rack-to-rack “after.”

Variant B

Horizontal bands — before on top, after on bottom

Two thin full-bleed bands frame the section: the messy rack runs across the top (labeled “Before”), the clean after across the bottom (labeled “After”). The four rows sit on solid navy between them. Keeps text legibility high because the photos never sit behind the copy — they bracket it. Works well even with a weaker “after” photo, since the bands read as atmosphere, not proof.

Variant C

Ghost before + bright after window

The messy rack becomes a faint, desaturated ghost across the whole background (atmosphere, not proof). A single bright “after” image sits in a framed window on the right, captioned “After — LUCI head-end.” The contrast does the storytelling: the mess recedes, the fix is the focal point. Text stays on the left over the ghost. Good when the “after” is one strong photo you want to feature.

Variant D

Before/after thumbnail chips (minimal change)

Keeps the current design almost as-is — rack bleeding in from the right, text on the left — but adds two small labeled thumbnail chips pinned to the right edge: a “Before” chip (the rack) and an “After” chip (the clean stack), with a mint arrow between them. The chips do the explicit before/after work without restructuring the section. Lowest-effort fix; the ambient rack stays, but it’s now captioned as the “before.”

Variant E

Drag-to-reveal slider in the background

A before/after slider lives in the background plane — before rack on the left, clean after on the right, with a draggable mint handle. Text sits in a solid navy panel on the left third so it’s always legible no matter where the slider sits. The most literal “before/after,” and interactive. Needs a static fallback for print/PDF (the slider parks at 50%). Best when the “after” is a strong rack-to-rack counterpart.

Variant F

Labeled before with mint “After” reveal strip

The messy rack is the full-bleed background (darkened, labeled “Before”). A bright mint-edged vertical strip on the right ~25% reveals the “after” image, labeled “After — LUCI head-end.” It reads as the fix arriving into the frame. Text on the left over the before. A middle-ground between C (ghost + window) and A (split): the before stays present as atmosphere, the after is a deliberate reveal, not a faint ghost.
