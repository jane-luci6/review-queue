# New LUCI What’s New one-pager — page-flow options

**For Jane to choose · 17 September 2026**

## What every flow holds

This is one 8.5 × 11-inch benefit argument, not a compressed release guide. Every option opens with **Putting the power of programming in your hands**, establishes that more customer control still comes with a LUCI team that stays, and uses the approved features only as proof of three benefits:

1. **Control the room** — Venue panels · Staging · Audio group control
2. **Control your view** — Customizable interface · Live map flexibility · Video/LED wall layout sync
3. **Control your security** — Audit trails · Live monitoring · Sign-in/session · In-product support

Every option ends in guided fulfillment: a named LUCI contact, a thin account-specific plan, and a shipped laptop. None uses a download CTA.

## A — The control arc

**Reading order, top to bottom**

1. What’s New announcement and the theme.
2. One short bridge: the property team gets a more direct path from decision to action, with LUCI still beside the operation.
3. **Control the room** — the immediate operating benefit, followed by its three proof labels.
4. **Control your view** — the property-fit benefit, followed by its three proof labels.
5. **Control your security** — visibility, accountability, and supported response, followed by its four proof labels.
6. Named-contact next step and the three-part fulfillment path.

**Above the fold / first read**

The upper third carries the announcement, theme, partnership bridge, and the start of **Control the room**. The reader understands the release promise before seeing an inventory.

**Pillar architecture**

The pillars form one descending argument rather than three equal columns: act in the space → shape what the team sees → know and govern what is happening. Each pillar gets one benefit sentence first; feature names follow as a compact proof line. The changing feature count does not force unequal boxes.

**CTA placement**

A compact closing band immediately after **Control your security**. It names the person, then shows: **Confirm the plan → Receive the laptop → Coordinate activation with LUCI.**

Sample wording: **Plan your New LUCI upgrade with [NAME].**

This is not a download, self-install, or generic “learn more” CTA.

**Stays off-page**

The Upgrade Guide + FAQ, feature mechanics, fulfillment detail beyond the three-step path, Add any endpoint, Display model catalog, compatibility answers, setup instructions, and technical security detail.

**Risk**

The third pillar may feel denser because it carries four proof features. The layout will need to keep those names subordinate so security does not become the page’s technical appendix.

## B — Three decisions

**Reading order, top to bottom**

1. Theme and a short statement of the release shift.
2. Three operator questions in sequence:
   - What needs to happen in the room?
   - What does the team need to see?
   - What needs to be known, governed, or escalated?
3. Each question resolves into its matching Control pillar and benefit.
4. A single partnership line connects in-product support to the LUCI team.
5. Named contact and fulfillment path close the page.

**Above the fold / first read**

The theme and all three questions appear in the first-read field. Before reading any feature names, the reader sees the range of control New LUCI puts in the team’s hands.

**Pillar architecture**

Each pillar is the answer to an operating decision, not a category. **Control the room** answers action; **Control your view** answers orientation; **Control your security** answers accountability and response. Features appear beneath each answer as evidence, with no standalone feature section.

**CTA placement**

The CTA follows the partnership line, so the guided route feels like the continuation of the control story rather than a sales footer.

Sample wording: **Talk with [NAME] about the plan for your property.**

The supporting line may name the thin plan, laptop, and coordinated activation. It is not a guide-download CTA.

**Stays off-page**

The Guide/FAQ, expanded workflows, complete fulfillment sequence, IT-only Add any endpoint and Display model catalog, sign-in methods, audit mechanics, diagnostic detail, and universal timing or compatibility promises.

**Risk**

Questions consume more words than labels. If they are not exceptionally tight, the page will trade useful proof for rhetorical setup and may feel less like a release announcement.

## C — Promise, proof, path

**Reading order, top to bottom**

1. Theme and one partnership sentence.
2. A three-line promise index: **Control the room · Control your view · Control your security**.
3. A proof ledger that moves through the pillars in the same order: one benefit statement, then the associated feature names for each.
4. A distinct “Your path to New LUCI” close: named person, thin plan, laptop, coordinated activation and support.

**Above the fold / first read**

The theme, partnership promise, and all three pillar names occupy the first-read field. A reader who only scans the top still receives the complete benefit structure.

**Pillar architecture**

The three pillars appear twice at different depths: first as a compact promise index, then as ordered proof rows. This separates the memorable control message from the feature evidence without turning the sheet into three cards or columns.

**CTA placement**

The bottom section is a short path, not merely a button. The named contact leads directly into the account plan, laptop, and activation sequence.

Sample wording: **Start with [NAME]. We’ll confirm the plan and coordinate what comes next.**

It is not a download, version-selection, or self-service route.

**Stays off-page**

The Upgrade Guide + FAQ, fulfillment responsibilities and timing, configuration detail, Add any endpoint, Display model catalog, technical implementation, complete feature descriptions, and proof claims not already approved.

**Risk**

Repeating the pillar names can feel redundant on a one-page sheet. The proof ledger must add meaning through benefit language, not simply restate the index.

## Recommendation

Choose **A — The control arc**.

It is the cleanest letter-fit because every element advances once: promise, partnership, three benefits, proof, then action. The pillar order naturally expands the control theme from the room to the interface to accountability, while the named-contact close makes continuing LUCI involvement part of the argument rather than a disclaimer.
