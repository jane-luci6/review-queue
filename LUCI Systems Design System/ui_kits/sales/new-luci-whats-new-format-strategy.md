# New LUCI What’s New — format strategy

**Research memo for Jane · 17 September 2026**

## The answer in one sentence

Keep the one-pager, but do not ask it to carry the release detail: customers should go to a maintained, web-first **New LUCI Upgrade Guide + FAQ** at one stable URL, while the webinar shows the release in use and the email program routes each audience to the right next step.

That is a hybrid play, not a choice between print and web. The one-pager carries the theme and three pillars. The guide carries the durable detail. The webinar carries demonstration and Q&A. Email carries timing and audience context.

## What comparable releases do

### Research method

I reviewed official public vendor sources for seven named releases in control, collaboration, and digital-signage systems. Each product joins software with dedicated or supported hardware. The review looked for launch pages, brochures or data sheets, release notes and guides, webinars or demonstrations, and visible differences between buyer and installed-customer paths.

“No one-pager surfaced” means no release-specific one-page PDF appeared in the official public material reviewed; it is not a claim that no private sales sheet existed.

## What the evidence says

1. **The launch asset and the reference asset do different jobs.** Every comparable release used more than one destination. None made a webinar the only durable source of feature detail.
2. **Short PDFs sell the shape of a system, not its full change log.** BrightSign, Atlona, and Barco use brochures, data sheets, or concise release PDFs for orientation and selection. Their procedures, compatibility, and release histories live elsewhere.
3. **Web wins for changing detail.** Q-SYS, Cisco, Atlona, SpinetiX, and Barco maintain versioned help, release notes, or firmware pages because upgrade information changes after launch.
4. **Webinars and demos translate; they do not archive.** They show workflows, create urgency, answer questions, and capture interest. Searchable guides remain the reference after the event.
5. **Customer and prospect paths split after the common story.** Prospects are routed toward overview, demonstration, trial, or sales. Installed customers and integrators are routed toward compatibility, upgrade, setup, and support.

The practical implication for LUCI: the one-pager can stay short only if the campaign names and maintains a next destination. “Ask your LUCI contact” is not enough; it turns every detail request into a manual sales follow-up.

## LUCI format options

### Option A — One-pager leave-behind only

**What it is:** Keep the 8.5 × 11-inch announcement sheet as the complete public artifact. Sales or the embedded team supplies detail when asked.

**Upside:** Portable, printable, easy to attach, easy to use in a meeting, and disciplined around one memorable argument.

**Tradeoff:** It does not answer the question in this memo. Customers have no durable self-serve reference, feature explanations get repeated inconsistently, and a changing beta release will outrun a static sheet.

**Verdict:** Keep the format, but not as the whole system.

### Option B — Longer “What’s New” webpage

**What it is:** Turn the release into one long public marketing page containing the narrative, feature sections, captures, webinar, and technical explanation.

**Upside:** Searchable, measurable, easy to update, and able to hold media without print compression.

**Tradeoff:** It mixes buyer persuasion with customer upgrade mechanics, makes the public release page carry information that will change through beta, and duplicates the roles already assigned to the webinar and upgrade guide. It also cuts against the current campaign guardrail not to create a dedicated public release page before auditing the pages LUCI already has.

**Verdict:** Do not replace the one-pager with this.

### Option C — Detail hub + short teaser

**What it is:** Keep the one-pager short and send customers to a maintained feature and upgrade reference.

**Upside:** The handout stays memorable while customers get searchable detail. A stable URL can be updated through beta, deep-linked from emails, and turned into a downloadable PDF snapshot when the release settles.

**Tradeoff:** The guide needs a named owner, a change process, and a clear access decision: public, unlisted, or customer-only.

**Verdict:** Strong foundation, but it needs the webinar to make the new workflows tangible.

### Option D — Webinar-led teaser

**What it is:** Make the one-pager primarily a registration device. Most feature explanation happens live.

**Upside:** Gives the campaign an event, captures interest, supports demonstration and Q&A, and creates follow-up material from the recording.

**Tradeoff:** Registration friction withholds useful information from current customers. The event date expires, recordings are slow to search, and people who cannot attend still need an authoritative reference.

**Verdict:** Use webinar leadership for attention, not for sole ownership of the detail.

### Option E — Hybrid: announcement + reference + demonstration

**What it is:** One-pager, web-first Upgrade Guide + FAQ, webinar, and email operate as one system with distinct jobs.

**Upside:** Matches the pattern across comparable releases; preserves the existing one-pager work; gives customers a durable answer; gives prospects a demonstration path; and lets detail evolve without rewriting the campaign thesis.

**Tradeoff:** Requires one canonical detail source and disciplined linking so parallel assets do not become competing versions.

**Verdict:** Recommended.

## Recommendation — the New LUCI hybrid

### 1. Keep the one-pager as the shortest complete argument

Its content hierarchy remains:

1. **Theme:** Putting the power of programming in your hands
2. **Three pillars:** Operate · Make it yours · See and secure
3. **Selected proof:** enough named evidence to make each pillar credible
4. **One next action:** wording and destination treatment to be locked later

Do not move setup steps, permission logic, filters, exports, sign-in methods, incident mechanics, or the technical catalog back onto the sheet.

### 2. Make the Upgrade Guide + FAQ the detail destination

Build it **web-first at one stable customer-facing URL** because beta details and upgrade guidance will change. It can offer a downloadable PDF snapshot after the release details settle, but the web version remains canonical.

This is a customer reference, not a long public marketing release page. It should contain:

- The same theme and three pillars as orientation
- A complete feature index under those pillars
- Feature behavior and use cases
- What changes for existing customers
- Readiness, prerequisites, permissions, compatibility, rollout, and upgrade steps
- IT and A/V detail
- FAQ, known limits, support route, and “last updated” date
- Webinar registration before the event, then the recording afterward

The guide should use anchors so customer emails can deep-link to the relevant section. It must not invent a new campaign story.

### 3. Use the webinar to show, not store

Structure the webinar in the same order:

1. Operate
2. Make it yours
3. See and secure

Demonstrate the primary proof feature in each pillar, then handle deeper mechanics and IT questions in Q&A. Before the event, the guide links to registration. After the event, the same place links to the recording and keeps the written answer beside it.

### 4. Let email route the audience

- **Existing customers:** lead to the Upgrade Guide + FAQ for what changes and how to prepare; use the webinar as the walkthrough and Q&A.
- **Prospects:** lead to the webinar or guided demo for the release story; use selected guide sections only when they help evaluation.
- **Sales follow-up:** use the one-pager as the leave-behind and the guide URL as the answer to detailed questions.

One common theme and pillar structure is enough. The routing can change by email audience without producing separate one-pager narratives.

## Explicit answer: where does the customer go for more detail?

**The customer goes to the New LUCI Upgrade Guide + FAQ at one stable URL.**

That page is the searchable, current source for feature behavior, upgrade readiness, permissions, compatibility, rollout, IT and A/V detail, known limits, and support. It also points to the webinar registration or recording for a visual walkthrough. The one-pager does not need to reproduce that material; it needs to make the destination unmistakable.

## What Jane should decide next — format lock only

Lock or reject these four format decisions:

1. **Hybrid system:** one-pager + web-first Upgrade Guide/FAQ + webinar + email.
2. **Canonical detail source:** the web guide is current; any PDF is a dated derivative.
3. **Webinar role:** demonstration and Q&A, not the only place feature detail exists.
4. **Audience routing:** customer email prioritizes the guide; prospect email prioritizes webinar/demo; the one-pager remains one shared campaign asset.

After that format lock, later passes can decide the guide’s access model and location, CTA wording, release date, capture selection and anonymization, webinar date, registration fields, and any approved proof. No one-pager copy or HTML needs to change to make the format decision.
