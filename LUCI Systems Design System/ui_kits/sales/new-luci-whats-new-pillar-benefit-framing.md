# New LUCI — benefit-led pillar framing

**Strategy options for Jane · 17 September 2026**

## The message to strengthen

**Putting the power of programming in your hands.**

For decades, casino A/V teams have had to route ordinary decisions through a chain of technology vendors and specialists. The problem is not that the team lacks judgment. It is that the operation has denied them a direct path from deciding to doing.

New LUCI moves that line. The people running the property can put control where the work happens, prepare and execute changes, shape the system around their environment, identify a problem, and act with better information.

The partnership promise stays intact: **more control does not mean less LUCI support**. The external story is control with a team that stays—not independence from LUCI, fewer reasons to call LUCI, or support being withdrawn.

## What needs to change

**Operate · Make it yours · See and secure** sorts the features correctly, but the labels read as categories. The pillar names should carry more of the customer outcome before the feature copy begins.

The proof mapping should not change:

| Pillar position | Proof that stays together |
|---|---|
| 1 | Venue panels · Staging · In-product support |
| 2 | Customizable interface · Live map flexibility |
| 3 | Audit trails · Live monitoring · Sign-in and session control |

The technical-detail list remains outside the three campaign pillars. Renaming a pillar does not move endpoint management, private tunnel, scoped diagnostic capture, or other technical items into the one-pager story.

## Option 1 — Decision to action

### 1. Decide. Then execute.

**Benefit argument:** Put control in the room, prepare the next move while the current one runs, apply it on cue, and bring LUCI in with context when help is needed.

### 2. Make the system yours.

**Benefit argument:** Shape the interface and live map around the property’s identity and physical space.

### 3. See. Trace. Control.

**Benefit argument:** See live state, trace what changed, and control who is active in the system.

**How it serves the theme:** This option turns programming power into a sequence of actions. It says the customer can decide and carry the decision through, then makes customization and visibility part of the same control story. The first argument also keeps LUCI visibly present at the point of escalation.

**Risks:** “Execute” is more formal than conversational, though it is already part of LUCI’s approved control language. “Decide. Then execute.” could imply going it alone if it appears without the partnership line. “See. Trace. Control.” is strong but needs its proof immediately below it so “control” does not sound generic.

## Option 2 — Control where work happens

### 1. Control in the room.

**Benefit argument:** Give the people running the space the controls, staging, and direct support route they need where the work is happening.

### 2. Built around your property.

**Benefit argument:** Bring the property’s identity, floor plans, orientation, and operating cues into the interface.

### 3. Visibility with accountability.

**Benefit argument:** Know what is happening now, what changed, what triggered it, and who is active.

**How it serves the theme:** This is the clearest institutional version. It replaces abstract buckets with three concrete benefits: local control, property fit, and accountable visibility. It also follows the feature mapping very tightly.

**Risks:** The labels are longer and less kinetic. “Control in the room” can make the first pillar sound limited to venue panels unless staging and support appear directly underneath. “Visibility with accountability” may feel more IT-led than operator-led.

## Option 3 — Conduct the operation

### 1. Call the next move.

**Benefit argument:** Prepare the change, put it into the room on cue, and reach LUCI from the work itself when support is needed.

### 2. Tune it to the property.

**Benefit argument:** Make the interface and live map reflect the place, the floor plan, and the way the team recognizes status.

### 3. Keep the whole picture.

**Benefit argument:** See the live operation, follow the record behind it, and govern active access.

**How it serves the theme:** This option carries the energy of the conductor narrative: the property team coordinates the whole operation instead of calling separate specialists for every move. It gives the release a more distinctive verbal identity without changing the proof.

**Risks:** The metaphor can overtake the product. “Tune” may be mistaken for audio tuning, and “whole picture” is less precise than the audit, monitoring, and session proof. This direction would require more supporting copy to stay declarative, so it is the least efficient option for the one-pager.

## Recommendation

Choose **Option 1 — Decision to action**.

It most directly completes the theme: programming power is not just access to controls; it is the ability to decide, execute, shape, see, trace, and govern. The verbs give the one-pager a benefit argument before the feature names arrive, while the proof remains faithful to the approved feature list.

Use one partnership bridge near the pillar sequence so the first label cannot be read as withdrawal:

> More control in your hands, with the LUCI team still there when you need us.

That line is a framing recommendation, not locked final copy. The final wording should preserve both halves: customer agency and continuing LUCI partnership.

## External voice guardrail

Do not promise:

- Less need for the LUCI team
- Fewer reasons to call LUCI
- Self-service as a replacement for support
- A property left to operate or troubleshoot alone

Do promise:

- More direct control over day-to-day decisions
- A shorter path from decision to execution
- Better context when the team does involve LUCI
- Continuing support from a team that stays

## Jane’s lock

Jane still chooses the pillar option and final language. Until then, keep the current feature mapping and do not update the one-pager HTML, campaign rule, or feature-list JSON.
