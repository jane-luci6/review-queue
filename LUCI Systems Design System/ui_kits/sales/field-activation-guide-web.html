For the team driving trip frequency, time on device, on-property spend, and the promotions that move them.

Every screen on the property is now yours to program — by zone, by daypart, in real time. The reach that used to live on a handful of dedicated signs now covers the whole floor.

01

Push a message to every screen in under two minutes.

With LUCI, you can push a marketing message from your own device to the whole floor or a single set of zones, and it’s live in moments. Then it drops back to the scheduled plan when you’re done.

  • React while the moment still matters, instead of after it passes
  • No ticket, no call, no waiting on another team’s queue
  • Every screen stays current — none stranded on yesterday’s message

One-time setup — get the message into LUCI as a channel

  1. In your digital signage CMS, open your media library and drag your message asset in (or pull it from a linked OneDrive/Canva). Set a validity end-date if it’s temporary so it auto-removes.
  2. Create a playlist, add the message to it, and assign the playlist to a signage player. That player’s output feeds a LUCI IP encoder, so the message now appears as a channel in LUCI’s source list.
  1. Open LUCI in your browser from any device and log in.
  2. On the property map, zoom out until every display on the floor is in view.
  3. Use the lasso tool to select every TV on the property — or select just the zones you want to reach.
  4. In the source list, pick the channel carrying your message. Every selected display flips to it immediately, live, with no ticket or call.
  5. To drop back: set a revert time (e.g., 60 seconds) and the floor returns to its scheduled content on its own — or apply your “normal” preset when you’re done.

02

Daypart your restaurant promotions to catch guests at the decision point.

Schedule a dining spot to run across the floor in the hour before lunch and again before dinner — the windows when guests are deciding whether to stay on property or head out — then reset the regular rotation automatically. Set the timing once and it repeats every day.

  • Capture covers that would otherwise walk out the door
  • Reach guests anywhere on the floor, not just the screens by the restaurants
  • Lift on-property spend in the same move

One-time setup — same as use case 01

  1. Load the dining spot in your digital signage CMS, put it on a playlist, and assign to a signage player so it appears as a channel in LUCI.
  1. In LUCI, open Presets and create a new preset (e.g., “Pre-lunch dining spot”).
  2. Add the displays you want to reach — the floor-wide set, not just the screens near the restaurants. Select them on the map or from the venue list.
  3. For each display in the preset, set its target to the dining spot channel.
  4. Save the preset.
  5. Open the Event Scheduler. Add the preset to the calendar in the hour before lunch (and a second entry in the hour before dinner). Set it to repeat daily, or pick specific days.
  6. Set a revert time on each entry (e.g., one hour) so the floor drops back to regular rotation automatically — or schedule a “normal” preset to follow it.

This week →Build a pre-lunch dining spot. Then stand on the floor at 11am and watch it run.

03

Put your video wall to work for marketing: run it full or split it into zones.

A video wall is the focal point of a space — the sportsbook, the bar, the entrance. With LUCI, set it up as a set of layouts (presets): one runs the full wall as one big display; another carves out side panels for promo ads, odds, or branding. Switch between them with a tap, or schedule the switch for the moment it matters.

  • One wall, multiple layouts — switch with a tap or on a schedule
  • Carve out zones for marketing without giving up the main feed
  • Reconfigure the layout yourself — no integrator visit

Before you start

  1. The video wall’s displays are already on LUCI’s map, and you have a processor (e.g., NovaStar, CORIOMaster) or a custom layout in mind.
  1. In LUCI, go to the property map, right-click the wall’s location, and choose Add Video Wall (Administration → Device Setup, Video).
  2. Step 1: pick the processor, name the wall, and assign it to a venue. For a custom wall with no processor, choose the rows and columns that match your layout.
  3. Choose the presets (layouts) you want for this wall — e.g., one preset for the full wall, another that carves out side panels — and rename them so each layout is clear.
  4. Step 2: for each preset, tie its screen-zones to the wall’s physical displays — select a screen on the preset, then the endpoint (display) it should drive. Repeat for every preset, then Save.
  5. Switch between layouts from the map with a tap, or schedule the switches (see Preset Events) for the moment it matters.
  6. To adjust later, right-click the wall and choose Edit Video Wall — re-tie zones, add a preset, or change the layout — no integrator visit.

This week →Pick one video wall and set up two presets: full-wall for the main event, and a zoned layout that carves out a panel for promo ads or branding. Switch between them once to feel it.

04

Build an event campaign that escalates automatically.

Schedule a rotation that grows an event’s presence — more zones, more frequency — as the date approaches, and fills your slow dayparts with loyalty and dining offers in between. Build it once; it ramps and resets on its own.

  • Build momentum toward an event without touching it daily
  • Put historically slow windows to work instead of waiting them out
  • Return the floor to normal automatically, with no stale content left running

One-time setup — content + frequency

  1. In your digital signage CMS, load the event promo asset(s). For higher frequency closer to the event, build a playlist that rotates the promo more often — frequency is controlled on the CMS side, zone coverage on the LUCI side.
  1. In LUCI, build a series of presets, each wider than the last: Preset A = one zone (e.g., lobby) on the event channel; Preset B = lobby + concourse; Preset C = full floor on the event channel.
  2. Open the Event Scheduler and place Preset A on the calendar for two weeks out, Preset B for one week out, and Preset C for the week of the event. Each can repeat daily during its window.
  3. To fill slow dayparts between campaign windows, schedule a separate preset that pushes loyalty and dining offers during historically slow windows.
  4. Schedule a “return to baseline” preset for the day after the event (or set revert times on Preset C) so no stale event content keeps running.
  5. Build it once — the calendar ramps and resets on its own from there.

For the team responsible for floor performance, guest experience, and the energy that keeps players on device.

The floor environment — sound, visuals, lighting, energy — now runs on purpose: matched to the daypart, the crowd, and the moment.

01

Set your floor’s audio to match the room’s energy, all day.

Build an audio schedule that moves the way your floor does — lighter in the morning, building through the afternoon, peaking in the evening, and easing back after the late-night crowd thins. Each zone follows its own curve, and the transitions happen on their own.

  • Keep the room feeling alive without a manager riding the faders
  • Match energy to the crowd you actually have at each hour
  • Give every shift the same intended sound

Before you start

  1. Q-SYS is the processor LUCI drives. If you’re on Biamp today, LUCI swaps in a Q-SYS processor and reuses your existing amps and sources — confirm the swap with your FDE.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the property map; navigate to the audio zone you want to adjust.
  2. Select the zone’s source — which channel or feed plays in that zone — from the source list.
  3. Set the zone’s volume. LUCI controls volume per venue, not inside the DSP.
  4. To keep announcements clean, assign mics to priority layers: LUCI’s three-layer ducking lets you set security high, page/valley-mic low, and so on — when the high-priority source comes on, it ducks the low.
  5. Adjust the independent volume of each priority per zone (e.g., the restaurant gets security only).
  6. Save the zone’s settings; changes apply in real time.
  7. To shift energy by daypart, build a preset that recalls this zone’s source and volume (upbeat at night, calm in the morning) and schedule it — see Gaming UC03.

This week →Pick one zone, set its daypart curve (calm morning, peak evening), and let the schedule carry it for a week — no one rides the faders.

02

Make every jackpot a floor event, automatically.

When a jackpot hits, a coordinated preset fires in that zone — accent lights pulse, audio swells for a beat, nearby screens flash a celebration — then everything resets on its own. The response is calibrated per area so it fits the space. If the jackpot preset doesn’t already exist, the guide below walks you through building one from scratch.

  • Turn one guest’s win into energy the whole floor feels
  • Create a sense of action that keeps nearby players engaged
  • Deliver it every time, without any manual effort

Before you start

  1. LUCI’s scripting engine is connected to your gaming system so it can listen for progressives and jackpots, and the celebration content (video + audio + lighting scene) is loaded as a channel/preset. Confirm the gaming-system integration is wired for your property.
  1. Load the jackpot celebration video as a channel and the celebration sound as an audio WAV in the zone — see Marketing UC01 for getting content into LUCI as a channel.
  2. Create the lighting scene for the celebration on your lighting processor (ETC Mosaic or Lutron); LUCI recalls it by pulling the scenes for that zone.
  3. In LUCI, build a preset that grabs the TVs in the jackpot zone, flips them to the celebration channel, triggers the audio WAV, and recalls the lighting scene — all in one preset.
  4. Connect LUCI’s scripting engine to your gaming system so it listens for the jackpot signal.
  5. Set the script to fire the preset the moment a jackpot hits — every TV in the zone flips to the celebration, the sound plays, the lights fire, automatically.
  6. Choose the scope: as mild as one TV, or as wild as every TV, audio zone, and lighting scene in the property.
  7. Optional: set a revert so the floor returns to its baseline after a set time.

This week →Build one celebration preset for your main pit, wire it to a test jackpot signal, and watch the zone fire on its own — sound, screens, and lights together.

03

Page the property without cutting the music.

LUCI’s announcement system uses priority ducking — a page overrides a zone’s audio for a moment, then the regular audio returns on its own. Page a guest, call a team member, or run a promo over the floor audio from any device, without a walk to the booth and without killing the music.

  • Page from anywhere — no walk to the booth, no dedicated mic
  • The music dips for the page, then comes back on its own
  • One system for guest pages, team pages, and promo spots

Before you start

  1. LUCI open on the property map, with the audio zones you want to page over already on it.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the property map.
  2. Pick the zone you want to page over — a single venue, a floor, or the whole property.
  3. Trigger the announcement from your phone or a mounted touchscreen. LUCI ducks the zone’s audio for the page, then brings the regular audio back on its own.
  4. Use it for a guest page (“your party is seated”), a team call, or a promo spot — no walk to the booth, no dedicated mic, no killing the music.
  5. Every page logs itself, so you have a record without a separate step.

This week →Next time a guest needs paging or a promo spot needs to run, do it from your phone over the floor audio — and notice you never had to leave the floor or cut the music.

04

Reconfigure the floor’s video wall for a tournament or event.

The big video wall on the floor isn’t stuck in one layout. Set it up as a set of presets — full wall for the tournament finale, zoned for leaderboards and branding — and switch between them with a tap, or schedule the switch for tip-off. When it’s over, one tap returns it to the everyday layout.

  • One wall, multiple layouts — switch with a tap or on a schedule
  • Full wall for the moment that matters, zoned for the rest
  • Reconfigure it yourself — no integrator visit

Before you start

  1. The video wall’s displays are already on LUCI’s map, and you have a processor (e.g., NovaStar, CORIOMaster) or a custom layout in mind.
  1. In LUCI, go to the property map, right-click the wall’s location, and choose Add Video Wall (Administration → Device Setup, Video).
  2. Step 1: pick the processor, name the wall, and assign it to a venue. For a custom wall with no processor, choose the rows and columns that match your layout.
  3. Choose the presets (layouts) you want — e.g., one preset for the full wall (tournament finale), another zoned for leaderboards and branding — and rename them clearly.
  4. Step 2: for each preset, tie its screen-zones to the wall’s displays. Repeat for every preset, then Save.
  5. On tournament day, switch to the full-wall preset for the finale and the zoned preset for the bracket rounds — or schedule the switches for tip-off (see Preset Events) so the wall runs itself.
  6. When it’s over, one tap returns the wall to the everyday layout — or schedule the revert so it happens on its own.

For the team responsible for keeping the property running.

The whole property’s audio and visual environment runs from one interface — every display, source, and zone, on or off site. Build it once, then manage by exception; the floor holds its baseline on its own, and your day shifts from execution to oversight.

01

Build the whole property’s schedule in one session, then let it run.

Spend some time configuring audio, displays, and zone behavior for every daypart, weekday and weekend, from one place. Once it’s built it runs itself, with a locked baseline underneath so the floor stays consistent no matter who opened — and you update any piece in real time without taking the rest offline.

  • Trade daily execution for oversight and the occasional adjustment
  • Eliminate the drift that creeps in shift to shift
  • Change one thing without disturbing anything else

Before you start

  1. LUCI open on the property map with the displays, audio zones, and (if used) lighting zones already on it.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the Presets side. You’ll build one preset per mode the property runs — normal weekday, Saturday peak, fight night, holiday, a private event in the ballroom.
  2. For each mode, click Add and name it, then grab the displays and audio zones it covers (use the lasso tool to select many at once on a touchscreen).
  3. Set what each should do at that mode: channel for each TV, source and volume for each audio zone, lighting scene if used, video-wall layout if used.
  4. Save each preset — they populate in the preset list as your library of modes.
  5. Open the Scheduling app and schedule each preset to its daypart: set the time, choose daily/weekly/monthly/custom, and save. Everything in the preset recalls at that time.
  6. Set a locked baseline preset underneath (e.g., the morning reset) so the floor always returns to a known state — the schedule holds the baseline on its own.
  7. To update one piece without disturbing the rest, edit that preset or zone in real time — the change applies live and the rest of the schedule keeps running.
  8. Step back: the whole property now runs itself day to day, and your day shifts from execution to oversight and the occasional adjustment.

This week →Build your two heaviest modes (weekday and Saturday peak) as presets, schedule them, and let the floor hold itself for a week — then notice what you stop touching.

02

Resolve service requests from wherever you are — including after hours.

A manager flags that the bar audio is off; you open that zone from your phone, adjust the level, and confirm it’s right — on property or off. Physical presence is reserved for genuinely physical problems, and every change logs itself.

  • Close out most issues in minutes, without a walk or a drive
  • Know whether a call really needs an on-site trip before anyone makes it
  • Keep an automatic record without a separate documentation step

Before you start

  1. LUCI is browser-based and runs on any device — Safari on iPad, a Surface, even a phone. You can reach it on or off property. Confirm you have remote access to LUCI from your phone.
  1. Get the request — a manager flags that the bar audio is off, or a zone needs a level change.
  2. Open LUCI in the browser on your phone (or tablet) from wherever you are, on or off property.
  3. Go to the property map and navigate to the zone the request is about.
  4. Adjust it: change the source, set the volume, or recall the zone’s baseline — the change applies in real time.
  5. Confirm it’s right — you can see the zone’s live state from the same screen.
  6. Decide whether it really needs an on-site trip. Most level and source issues close from the phone; reserve the walk for genuinely physical problems (a cable, a dead display).
  7. Every change logs itself — you have an automatic record without a separate documentation step.

This week →Next time a level request comes in, close it from your phone before you leave your desk — and check the log to confirm it recorded.

03

Swap any source without a truck roll.

When a channel changes, a feed needs updating, or you want to repurpose a screen, edit the source on the map yourself. Update it once and every display drawing from that source picks up the change — no integrator visit, no truck roll, no waiting on a vendor.

  • Change a channel or feed yourself, the same day you decide to
  • One edit propagates to every display using that source
  • Skip the integrator visit and the vendor wait

Before you start

  1. LUCI open on the property map, with the source you want to change already on the map.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the property map.
  2. Find the source you want to update — the channel, feed, or signage input feeding the displays in question.
  3. Edit the source: change the channel, swap the feed, or update the input. (The exact fields depend on the source type.)
  4. Save the change.
  5. Every display drawing from that source picks up the new content automatically — you don’t touch each screen.
  6. Confirm by glancing at the affected displays on the map, or walking one, to see the new content live.

This week →Next time a channel changes or a feed needs updating, edit the source yourself instead of calling the integrator — and watch it propagate to every screen using it.

04

Configure a private event once, then activate it and let it revert on its own.

Build the full A/V setup for a banquet, concert, or private event as a named preset — custom audio mix, branded screen content, zone-specific settings — and enter the number of minutes until it should revert. The preset runs for the event and returns the property to its baseline on its own, with no tap to switch it off. If you don’t already have a preset built, the guide below starts from scratch.

  • Deliver a polished event without rebuilding it each time
  • Hand activation to anyone — it’s one tap, not a checklist
  • Guarantee the floor returns to normal the moment it’s over

Before you start

  1. LUCI open on the property map with the displays, audio zones, and (if used) lighting zones for the event space already on it.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the Presets side; click Add and name the preset after the event (e.g., Saturday banquet, Corporate concert).
  2. Grab the displays and audio zones the event uses — the ballroom TVs, the event audio zone, any lighting zones.
  3. Set the full A/V setup in the preset: the custom audio mix (source and volume per zone), the branded screen content (flip the event TVs to the right channel), any zone-specific settings and lighting scene.
  4. Set the revert: enter the number of minutes the event should run before the property returns to its baseline.
  5. Save the preset — it sits in the preset list, ready to reuse for the next instance of the same event.
  6. At event time, activate it with one tap (or schedule it to the event’s start time). The preset runs for the event.
  7. When the timer ends, the property returns to its baseline on its own — no tap to switch it off, no risk the floor is left in the event state overnight.

This week →Build your next banquet as a named preset with a revert timer, hand activation to anyone (it’s one tap), and watch the floor return to baseline on its own.

A/V is now standard infrastructure on the network you already run — visible, governed, and logged like everything else. No proprietary black boxes, no specialist truck roll to find out what’s happening.

01

Add an endpoint to LUCI — video, audio, or lighting.

When a new display, speaker zone, or lighting system lands on your network, bring it onto LUCI yourself — no integrator truck roll. Enter the device’s IP, name it, assign it to a venue, and it’s on the map for every authorized user on that venue to reach.

  • Stand up new hardware yourself, on your schedule
  • One click-path covers video, audio, and lighting
  • Every endpoint you add is visible, tagged, and managed like the rest of the network

Before you start

  1. You have an admin PIN and the device’s IP address — it must match the field device (the set-top box/TV, the DSP, or the lighting system).
  1. Log in to LUCI with your admin PIN.
  2. Left menu → Administration → Map Modes → Device Setup [Administration].
  3. Select the device type: Video, Audio, or Lighting [Devices]. (On LUCI v2.34.0+, use the Endpoint Manager instead.)
  4. Navigate the map to where the endpoint lives.
  5. Right-click (or long-press) the map → Add Endpoint (for lighting, Add Device).
  6. Video only: pick the Brand from the dropdown. (Audio and lighting skip this.)
  7. Name the endpoint — this is the label shown on the UI.
  8. Notes: add asset tagging or other info you want to track.
  9. Device Address: enter the IP that matches the field device (video: the set-top box/TV; audio: the DSP; lighting: the lighting system).
  10. Assign the endpoint to a Venue so every user on that venue can reach it (unless you restrict it per user — see IT UC03).
  11. Video only: toggle Auto Power to let a watchdog keep the device powered on.
  12. Set any other relevant options, then click Save.
  13. To edit later: right-click the device on the map (or in the Assigned list) → Edit Endpoint.

This week →Add one new display (or audio or lighting endpoint) yourself, end to end, so the path is in your hands the next time a device lands.

  1. For config and backup files: open the LUCI Smartsheet your FDE shared with you — every config file and backup for your system lives there, fully documented.
  2. For user guides, admin guides, and walkthrough videos: open the LUCI support site.
  3. Before you open a ticket: walk the basic troubleshooting guides on the support site — a lot of issues you can clear yourself.

03

Define who can change what — by role, by zone, by team.

Set role-based permissions centrally so Marketing controls content, A/V controls schedules and levels, and no one reaches outside their lane — enforced by the platform and managed from a single administrative view.

  • Give each team exactly the access it needs, nothing more
  • Reduce the risk of an accidental change to the wrong zone
  • Adjust permissions in one place as roles change

Before you start

  1. You have an admin PIN. (The KB covers per-user source permissions; confirm with Mike whether zone-level and feature-level access — schedules, health checks — live in this same screen or elsewhere.)
  1. Log in to LUCI with your admin PIN.
  2. Navigate to User Management → Access.
  3. Select the user whose access you’re setting. Do this before touching any controls — permissions are edited per selected user.
  4. Open the Source tab. You’ll see every source in the system and this user’s current permission for each.
  5. For each source, set the View permission: Enabled (user can tune a display to it), Disabled (visible in the user’s list but can’t tune to it), or Hidden (not shown to this user at all).
  6. Set the Edit permission per source: On (user can edit that source’s details — edits apply to all users) or Off (read-only for this user).
  7. To set the same permission across every source at once, use the batch controls at the top right: the Options dropdown sets View for all sources, and the Edit All toggle sets Edit for all.
  8. For source-by-source control, use the dropdown and toggle next to each source individually.
  9. Repeat for each user. Permissions take effect immediately.

This week →Sit with one teammate and confirm their source access matches exactly what their role needs — tighten anything that’s too broad.

04

See device health across the property at a glance.

Zoom out on the property map and LUCI clusters every endpoint into a single colored circle — green means the cluster is all online, yellow means at least one device is offline, red means at least one is powered off. Click a cluster to break it apart and see exactly which device needs attention, so a dead screen gets caught before a guest ever notices it.

  • Spot offline and powered-off devices before a guest does
  • Read the whole property’s health in one glance, not zone by zone
  • No polling or walk-arounds — the map tells you where to look

Before you start

  1. LUCI v2.17.0 or later — clustering is on by default. (If you need it disabled, contact LUCI support.) A login with access to the property map.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the property map; zoom out until endpoints group into cluster circles, each with a count of the devices inside.
  2. Scan the property: green clusters are all online and powered on — no action needed.
  3. A yellow cluster means at least one device in that area is offline; a red cluster means at least one is powered off. If a cluster has both, it shows yellow — offline is treated as the more urgent.
  4. Click the yellow or red cluster (or scroll into it) to break it apart into its individual endpoints.
  5. Hover an endpoint — or long-press on a touchscreen — to see which venue it belongs to, so you know whose area is affected.
  6. Send someone to that device: power it back on or restore its connection before a guest hits a dead screen.
  7. Make a quick map scan part of the morning reset check or the A/V walkthrough, so problems get caught by attention, not by guest complaints.

This week →Zoom out on the map, find any yellow or red cluster, and send someone to fix the device behind it — before a guest ever notices. Then make that scan part of someone’s morning check.

The bigger picture

A/V stops being a vendor’s black box and becomes endpoints on your network — monitored, permissioned, and logged like every other system you run.

A/V moves from a capital line to a predictable platform investment — and, for the first time, becomes something you can measure. The cost structure your old A/V budget was built around no longer applies.

01

Replan the capital you’d reserved for the next A/V refresh.

The proprietary hardware that needed a full refresh every five to seven years is replaced by a software platform on a predictable annual line. Capability now arrives through updates rather than capital purchases, which changes the refresh request that used to drive your A/V budget.

  • Free the capital you’d set aside for the next hardware cycle

  • Forecast A/V spend instead of bracing for the next big ask
02

Cut or renegotiate the vendor and integration contracts LUCI replaces.

A large share of A/V’s old operating cost lived outside the property, in vendor and integration contracts that existed because the expertise didn’t. That control is internal now, so the contracts written around the old limitations are yours to consolidate or end.

  • Recover spend on scopes your team now handles directly
  • Consolidate overlapping vendors into one managed relationship
  • Retire contracts built around limitations that no longer exist
03

Start measuring A/V as a revenue contributor, not just a cost.

When marketing runs dining dayparts, loyalty pushes, and event countdowns through LUCI, that activity becomes part of a traceable campaign. Pair it with your performance data and you can compare campaign windows against baseline periods instead of treating A/V as a flat facility cost.

  • Build a defensible link between A/V activity and results
  • Evaluate A/V spend alongside marketing, not just facilities
  • Give ownership a revenue story, not just an expense line
04

Document and defend your A/V spend with platform data.

For the first time there’s an auditable record beneath the A/V investment — what’s running, what it costs, and how it’s used across teams. When ownership asks for justification, the utilization and performance data is already there.

  • Answer “what are we getting for this?” with real numbers
  • Tie spend to specific capabilities and outcomes, not overhead
  • Walk into budget conversations with evidence in hand

The bigger picture

For the first time, A/V is a number you can manage and defend.

01

Walk the floor with an iPad and take the controls yourself.

LUCI runs from any device. On your next walkthrough, open the dashboard on your device of choice and make a live change — flip a screen, adjust a zone’s audio, switch a preset — from wherever you’re standing, and confirm that what the platform reports matches what you see in person.

  • See and shape the guest experience without leaving the floor
  • Verify the floor is running the way it’s supposed to, live
  • Build your own fluency instead of relying on one operator

Before you start

  1. LUCI is browser-based and runs on any device — Safari on iPad works especially well. Confirm you have a login with control of the zones you’ll touch.
  1. On your next walkthrough, open LUCI in Safari on the iPad and log in.
  2. Go to the property map and zoom into the area you’re standing in — it unnests the individual endpoints so you can see each display and zone around you.
  3. Pick something to change live: flip a screen to a different channel, adjust a zone’s audio level, or switch a preset.
  4. Make the change from the iPad — it applies in real time.
  5. Look up from the screen and confirm what LUCI reports matches what you actually see on the floor.
  6. Use the lasso tool to select several displays at once if you want to change a whole section in one gesture.
  7. Repeat through the walk: each live change builds your own fluency so you’re not relying on a single operator to read the floor.

This week →Take an iPad on your next walkthrough, make one live change from the floor, and confirm it with your own eyes — that’s the whole point.

02

Schedule a morning reset so the floor always wakes up looking right.

A TV gets flipped to local news, a screen gets left on the wrong input — it happens. A scheduled reset returns every display to its assigned content during the quiet 5–7am window, before morning traffic picks up — with no one checking and nothing left to chance.

  • Start every day with a floor that’s already correct
  • Erase last night’s stray changes automatically
  • Free your team from a manual morning checklist

Before you start

  1. LUCI open on the property map with the displays, audio zones, and (if used) lighting zones already on it.
  1. Open LUCI and go to the Presets side; click Add and name it Morning reset.
  2. Grab every display in the property (use the lasso tool, or select-all) and set each to its assigned baseline channel.
  3. Grab every audio zone and set each to its baseline source and volume.
  4. If you use lighting, add the lighting zones and recall the morning scene.
  5. Save the preset — it’s now your single source of ‘what right looks like’ each morning.
  6. Open the Scheduling app and schedule the Morning reset to run every day in the 5–7am window (set the time, choose daily, save).
  7. Each morning the floor returns to its baseline on its own — last night’s stray changes (a TV on local news, a screen on the wrong input) are erased before anyone checks.
  8. Walk the floor once morning traffic picks up and confirm it’s correct — then stop running a manual morning checklist.

This week →Build the Morning reset preset, schedule it for every day, and on Monday walk in to a floor that’s already correct — no checklist, no chasing stray screens.

03

Schedule a recurring guest-experience strategy meeting with the whole team.

The floor performs best when teams plan it together. Put a standing session on the calendar where Marketing, Operations, A/V, and IT decide how the floor builds energy, which messages run where, and how it responds to events — then each team executes against the shared plan.

  • Turn a shared floor into a single, intentional strategy
  • Empower each team to move fast because everyone knows the plan
  • Catch conflicts before they play out in front of guests
04

Set LUCI to open to the zone you walk first.

Most General Managers walk the property on a familiar route each shift — the loop they take to read the floor. Configure LUCI to open the map right where that walk begins, and the dashboard meets you there on every login, with no panning or zooming to find your bearings. Each manager can save their own starting view, so every walkthrough starts where it should.

  • Open LUCI already oriented to the zone you care about
  • Give each manager their own starting view
  • Stop pinching and panning before every walkthrough

Before you start

  1. A LUCI login with access to the property map. Any user can save their own starting state — it lives in the browser, so each manager sets theirs on the device they walkthrough with.
  1. Open LUCI and navigate to the zone you walk first — pan and zoom until that area fills the view the way you want to see it each morning.
  2. Set the side bar, device type, and map mode the way you want them to open, so your starting state is fully shaped — not just the location.
  3. Open the main menu and choose Preferences.
  4. Under Saved State, select ‘Let me choose where to start,’ then click Save — LUCI now opens to this exact location, zoom, and layout on every login.
  5. Log out and back in to confirm LUCI opens right to your saved zone.
  6. After wandering elsewhere on the map, open Preferences and click Restore to snap back to your starting spot.
  7. Have each manager repeat this on their own login so every walkthrough begins oriented to the zone that manager owns.

This week →Save your starting map state to the zone you walk first, then log out and back in — LUCI should open right to it. No more pinching and panning to start the day.
