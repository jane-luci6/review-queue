# What’s New one-pager — field research (18 Sep 2026)

**Asset:** US Letter leave-behind, `new-luci-whats-new-onepager-flow-c.html`  
**Question:** how should name + description rows sit on a product screenshot — not how a newsletter should look.  
**Why this file exists:** the May 27 2026 “modern / edgy” pass was a web search for *editorial newsletters*. Search results were never saved. Those conclusions were applied to The Signal, then copied into house guidelines. They are not a source document for this sheet.

This note is the source. Sources and URLs are listed. Do not treat Issue 01’s `.svc-list` as the answer.

---

## 1. What this object is

A sales one-pager is one side of one sheet. Dock, LeadMagic, and GTM Playbook all describe the same job: a champion can skim it in **30–60 seconds** and forward it. It is not a sequential read. Everything is visible at once, so the first act is a **scan to decide where to start**, not a reading path. ([Playyy, Aug 2026](https://playyy.ai/blog/one-pager-design-ideas); [Dock](https://www.dock.us/library/sales-one-pagers); [GTM Playbook](https://discover.gtmplaybook.co/sales-one-pager-template); [LeadMagic](https://leadmagic.io/gtm-skills/one-pager-builder))

That is the opposite of a newsletter. A newsletter *controls sequence* (masthead → article → article). A one-pager *surrenders sequence*. Layout advice built for Issue 01 therefore fails here even when the typefaces and palette are the same.

| May 27 principle | Holds for this sheet? |
|---|---|
| Extreme type-size contrast | **Yes** — but as a *three-step scale on one canvas*, not ghost section numbers. |
| Section identifiers as visual anchors | **Yes** — the three promise names + `01/02/03`. Not a kicker + mint dash under every head. |
| Mint as structure, not decoration | **Yes** — mint on the numerals (and dark-header accents). Not on every feature name. |
| Every section a distinct visual register | **No.** One sheet has three *zones* (claim / evidence / close), not six magazine departments. The field is one register: copy over a live UI. |

Bloomberg / Wired / Monocle ghost numerals were named later to justify newsletter section numbers. They are editorial-magazine furniture. They are not a leave-behind pattern.

## 3. What 2026 research says for *this* format

Searched 18 Sep 2026: sales one-pager / leave-behind; product screenshot overlays; B2B datasheet typography; 2026 corporate brochure/print.

### 3.1 Zones before columns

Playyy (Aug 2026): split the sheet **horizontally first** — claim / evidence / action — roughly 25% / 55% / 20%. Columns only *inside* the evidence band. A page feels cluttered when claim, proof, and ask are interleaved.

This sheet already has that: dark mast (claim), light map field (evidence), close + navy foot (action). Keep it. Do not restyle the field as a newsletter article.

### 3.2 The screenshot is the proof, not wallpaper

GTM Playbook and Dock: do not use generic photography; use a **real product screenshot**. Borrowed Pen (med-device one-pagers): **one strong product image, not a collage**; benefits grouped and scannable; white space so the eye knows where to go.

The backwards-L (right rail + bottom control bar of the live map) is the correct 2026 move: argument on the left, proof occupying the rest of the field. Hairlines and table grids drawn *over* that UI compete with the product’s own lines.

### 3.3 Type is the structure

[Inkorporated, Feb 2026](https://www.theinkorporated.com/insights/corporate-brochure-design-trends-2026/): 2026 corporate print is **purpose-led minimalism** — fewer elements, each with a role; generous spacing; **typography as a structural device**. “If the hierarchy isn’t clear, the message won’t be either.”

[Helion360](https://helion360.com/blog/tech-startup-brochure-design-complex-layouts-typography) (tech brochures): three levels — display claim 36–48pt (we already have that in the mast), section entry **20–24pt**, body **9–11pt print**.

[Playyy](https://playyy.ai/blog/one-pager-design-ideas): three steps at ~**1.6 ratio**, e.g. **32 / 18 / 11pt** on letter. A headline at 20pt over body at 16pt does not read as a headline. **A fourth level almost always means the content wants a different structure.** Whitespace is a budget (25–35% empty), not leftover; space around a size jump is what makes the jump readable.

[Quarter Rest](https://www.quarterreststudios.com/how-to-design-a-product-brochure-that-converts-browsers-into-buyers/): hold **~30% white space**; two typefaces; body 9–11pt print.

[AtroPIM / catalog 2026](https://micro-printing.com/index.php/2026/05/22/how-to-design-a-catalog-for-print-the-2026-professional-guide/): **bold for names, regular for supporting detail** — contrast of *weight*, not extra chrome. Specs belong in tables; this sheet is not a spec sheet.

### 3.4 Features: groups, not lists

[Open Strategy Partners](https://openstrategypartners.com/blog/boost-your-sales-with-our-ultimate-guide-to-product-data-sheets/): organize features into **logical-functional groups** with a tagline for the group. The three promises *are* those groups. Features are members of a family, not a table of contents.

Dock/GTM still say “bullets” for skim speed. That is copy advice for teams with no type system. With Space Grotesk + Inter, **weight + size + proximity** replace bullets. Jane already rejected gold squares (bullets) and mint run-ins (still a list).

Gestalt: **small gap = same group; large gap = next group.** Rules are for when spacing cannot do the job. Repeating hairlines between every feature are a spec-table / Sharp-list signal. They also pick a fight with the screenshot.

### 3.5 Grid choice for *this* content

Playyy’s grid table:

| Grid | Best for |
|---|---|
| Single column | 150–250 words, 1 visual, one argument |
| Asymmetric 60/40 | claim + proof (screenshot in the narrow/remaining field) |
| Three-column band | three *parallel peers* (tiers, steps) in a shallow strip |
| Modular cards | dense mixed specs |

This sheet is **asymmetric 60/40 inside the evidence zone**: copy is the argument, map is the proof. A three-column band of the three promises would steal the screenshot and imply three equal products. Modular cards are what Jane already asked not to do.

F-pattern (Nielsen Norman, cited by Playyy): top-left is strongest; **bottom-right is weakest for text**. Putting the live UI in the bottom-right (the backwards L) puts proof where text would die anyway. That is correct. Do not pour more feature chrome into that corner.

Z-pattern only applies to *sparse* sheets (five or six elements). This field is text-dense enough to stay F-pattern: left edge must stay a clean scan.

### 3.6 What to ignore from generic one-pager templates

- Outcome stats / percentage proof (LUCI voice).
- Generic “Schedule a demo” CTA (locked close is information + URL).
- Icon-per-row feature tables (already tried; crowded).
- Soft-icon tiles from the Capabilities doc — that is a multi-page light document, not a screenshot field.
- Newsletter Sharp `dt` mint + hairline `dd` — editorial list furniture.

## 4. Diagnosis of the current field

| What’s on the sheet now | Why it fails this format |
|---|---|
| Feature name and teaser both 13px | No type step. Reads as a table, not a scan. |
| Mint on every `dt` | Mint is decorative. Structure should be `01/02/03` only. |
| `232px 1fr` + `border-bottom` | Spec ledger / Issue 01 `.svc-list`. Fights the map’s own UI lines. Dead space after short names (“Staging”). |
| Promise name 17px vs features 13px | Too close; Playyy’s “slightly larger body text.” |
| “The 3 Promises…” at 23px | Competes with the three promise names — the actual 30-second scan. |

## 5. Prescription for this sheet (apply next)

Locked: mesh header, gold on *programming*, no “What’s New” in header or field, no promise benefit lines, no header dashes, light map as field, backwards-L, fade around copy, copy as written.

**Change only the evidence-zone type and grouping.**

1. **Three type steps in the field, ~1.6 apart**
   - Promise name: Space Grotesk 700, ~20px (the scan).
   - Feature name: Space Grotesk 600, ~13px, **ink-strong** (not mint).
   - Teaser: Inter 400, ~12px, ink. Same line, to the right.
   - Chapter title *The 3 Promises of New LUCI*: smaller than the promises (~15–16px) so it labels the band and gets out of the way.
   - Numerals `01/02/03`: mint `#2b9e80`, ~24px — the only structural mint in the field.

2. **Proximity, not hairlines**
   - Delete feature-row borders.
   - Tight stack inside a promise (~8px).
   - Larger gap between promises (~32px).
   - Features hang under the promise *name*, not flush with the numeral (numeral = marker; copy = hanging block).

3. **Horizontal pair, not a 232px table**
   - `flex` + gap: name takes its natural width, teaser follows immediately.
   - Short names no longer sit across a void. Long names still sit left of their sentence.
   - Left-align; keep the F-pattern edge.

4. **Do not**
   - Restore gold squares, mint dashes under heads, icons, cards, or three equal columns.
   - Recolor feature names mint.
   - Copy Issue 01 `.svc-list`.
   - Add a fourth type level (kickers, captions, extra labels).
