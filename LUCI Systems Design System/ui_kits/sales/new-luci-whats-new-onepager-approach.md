# New LUCI What’s New one-pager — approach

## The job of the page

This is the campaign’s shortest complete argument: a letter-size handout that introduces New LUCI to existing customers and prospects, gives sales a credible leave-behind, and establishes the message the webinar, emails, and upgrade guide expand.

It should not summarize the whole release. It should make three promises memorable, show enough product evidence to make them real, and give the reader one next action.

## What survives one-sheet compression

Keep on the page:

- **Announcement:** What’s New, the release date once locked, and **Putting the power of programming in your hands**.
- **Orientation:** the existing benefit line and the single customers-and-prospects audience cue.
- **Three promises:** **Operate · Make it yours · See and secure**, each with its locked argument.
- **Primary proof:** Venue panels · Customizable interface · Live monitoring, with one short locked proof sentence each.
- **Breadth without explanation:** Staging · In-product support · Live map flexibility · Audit trails · Sign-in and session control may remain as compact names under their promise.
- **One action:** one CTA, not a menu of options.

Move off the page:

- Feature mechanics, setup steps, permission detail, filtering, export, incident flow, sign-in methods, and technical catalog items → **upgrade guide**.
- Full demonstrations, workflows, before/after operating moments, and technical Q&A → **webinar**.
- Release timing, audience-specific framing, reminders, and follow-up sequences → **email**.
- Any proof claim without an approved source → nowhere until it is substantiated.

The locked copy file should remain intact as campaign source material. Compression means selecting from it for this format, not rewriting it to fit.

## Hierarchy under space pressure

The page must read in this order: **narrative → three promises → proof → action**. The promises need equal strategic weight, but they do not need equal boxes, equal word counts, or equal-sized screenshots.

Use one continuous visual rhythm rather than three side-by-side columns. Let the theme own the first read; let the promise names and arguments form the second; let UI evidence form the third. Secondary feature names should behave like a proof index, not five additional mini-sections.

## Three approaches for GLM to develop later

### A — Announcement spine

- Keep the announcement and theme as the dominant moment; compress the benefit and audience cue into the opening field.
- Run the three promises as a top-to-bottom sequence, not equal cards.
- Give one proof feature per promise visual priority; reduce the other proof features to names only.
- Use one dominant UI capture plus two smaller, tightly cropped supporting views so screenshots do not consume three separate page sections.
- Omit the proof strip unless approved evidence exists; end with one compact CTA.

### B — Product window

- Make a single, strong New LUCI interface capture the page’s central evidence.
- Anchor all three promises to specific visible parts of that interface, preserving their names and arguments.
- Keep Venue panels, Customizable interface, and Live monitoring as the three labeled proof moments; move every supporting explanation off-sheet.
- Use the remaining feature names as a quiet footer index under their correct promise.
- Choose this only if one approved capture can honestly show enough of all three promises.

### C — Three-beat field guide

- Keep three distinct promise beats, each pairing its argument with one cropped UI view.
- Make the beats change scale and alignment across the sheet so they feel sequential, not like a comparison table.
- Keep only the primary proof sentence in each beat; move supporting proof descriptions to the webinar and upgrade guide.
- Use a shared proof-name rail and one shared CTA to prevent each beat from becoming a self-contained card.
- This is the most product-rich option, but it requires the strongest discipline on capture size and copy.

## Visual direction notes for GLM

- Build to one fixed **8.5 × 11-inch** sheet from the start. Use the sales print system’s safe margins, embedded brand fonts, fixed-sheet fit check, print-color handling, and pinned footer discipline.
- Treat this as one announcement sheet, not a compressed multi-page sales document. Use LUCI navy, off-white, mint-first accents, restrained gold, and the e-mesh as a quiet campaign texture—not another content zone.
- Keep body copy in Inter, structure in Space Grotesk, and reserve a single display moment. Favor whitespace and hairlines over stacked boxes.
- Use real New LUCI captures only. Crop to the action being proved, remove client-identifying data, keep UI text legible in print, and do not ship placeholder frames.
- Decide the capture system before layout: one hero plus two details, or three coordinated crops. Do not attempt three full application windows on one sheet.

## Decisions Jane still owns

- **CTA:** keep “See New LUCI in action.” or choose a more specific walkthrough/webinar action.
- **Release date:** lock the date before final print; the placeholder is not production copy.
- **Proof strip:** supply one approved anonymous proof point, or omit the strip entirely.
- **UI captures:** choose the final screens, their priority, and what must be anonymized.

**GPT recommendation:** Choose **A — Announcement spine**; it preserves the complete three-promise argument, feels like a release cornerstone rather than a feature sheet, and has the clearest path to one letter page.
