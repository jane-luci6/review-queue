Soft-icon duotone — current vs. alternatives

The canonized §5.1 house style uses mint-dark as the primary icon stroke on a pale tile with a gold accent. Retiring mint-dark on light surfaces means picking a replacement primary stroke. Same six icons, three treatments, same 64px tiles.

Now

Mint-dark + gold

Primary stroke mint-dark #2b9e80, gold #CEB06E accent, pale-mint tile. Today's house style across the cap doc, brochure & website.

Option A

Ink + gold

Primary stroke ink #1C2B33, gold #EDD086 accent, pale-gold tile. No mint on light; duotone preserved; icons stay high-contrast & legible.

Option B

Gold-primary (fails)

Primary stroke gold #EDD086, gold-deep #CEB06E accent, pale-gold tile. This is what "use gold for the stroke" literally produces — the icon nearly disappears (~1.3:1).

Read this as: "Now" is today's mint-dark duotone (the style you canonized in §5.1). "Option A" keeps a strong duotone with no mint on light — ink carries the icon, gold is the accent. "Option B" is the literal "gold stroke" reading and shows why a gold primary stroke doesn't work on a light tile. Note: "keep mint-dark for icon strokes only" (the other path) looks identical to "Now" — so the real choice is between Now/keep and Option A.
