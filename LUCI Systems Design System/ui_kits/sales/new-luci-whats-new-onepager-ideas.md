# New LUCI What’s New one-pager — structure and angle options

Ideas only. The finished handout should open with **Putting the power of programming in your hands** and organize all proof under **Operate · Make it yours · See and secure**.

**Locked content key for every option**
- **Operate:** “Control now lives in the venue, not just the back office.” Proof: Venue panels · Staging · In-product support
- **Make it yours:** “Your maps, your look — the system belongs to the property.” Proof: Customizable interface · Live map flexibility
- **See and secure:** “What’s happening now, what happened, and who’s in the system.” Proof: Audit trails · Live monitoring · Sign-in and session control

## Option 1 — Three promises

**Headline approach**  
Use Mike’s phrase as the dominant headline. Follow it immediately with the three pillar arguments so the theme resolves into three clear promises, not a release-feature inventory.

**How the three pillars land on one page**  
Three equal pillar columns. Each opens with the pillar name and its exact argument, gives one proof feature a little room, then names the remaining proof features in compact one-line form.

**Proof features**
- **Get air:** Venue panels · Customizable interface · Live monitoring
- **Stay one-liners:** Staging · In-product support · Live map flexibility · Audit trails · Sign-in and session control

**What NOT to cram**
- Feature-level bullet lists
- Setup, permission, filtering, export, or incident mechanics
- Technical-detail items
- Multiple examples for venue panels

**IT sidebar?**  
No. Equal pillar weight is the strength of this option; a sidebar would turn a clean campaign hierarchy back into a crowded feature sheet.

## Option 2 — From intent to proof

**Headline approach**  
Open with Mike’s phrase, then use each pillar as a short argument-and-evidence chapter. The page reads top to bottom as: what New LUCI puts in your hands, what that changes, and the features that prove it.

**How the three pillars land on one page**  
A narrative opening band followed by three stacked pillar bands in the locked order. Each band pairs the exact pillar argument with one vivid proof moment and a short supporting proof line; a compact footer names the remaining features under their pillars.

**Proof features**
- **Get air:** Staging · Venue panels · Customizable interface · Audit trails
- **Stay one-liners:** In-product support · Live map flexibility · Live monitoring · Sign-in and session control

**What NOT to cram**
- Every control available on a venue panel
- Preset and schedule mechanics
- Theme-adjustment settings
- Audit filters, CSV export, and incident lifecycle detail
- Technical-detail items beyond a possible sidebar

**IT sidebar?**  
Yes, but tiny. A four-item “For IT and A/V” strip can name Audio group control, Add any endpoint from LUCI, Video wall layout sync, and Central display model catalog without interrupting the release argument.

## Option 3 — One release, three views

**Headline approach**  
Treat Mike’s phrase as the page-wide lens. The headline introduces one release; the three pillars show what it means for the person running the venue, the property shaping the experience, and the team accountable for visibility and access.

**How the three pillars land on one page**  
A single central narrative spine with three anchored pillar sections around it. Each pillar keeps its exact name and argument, then uses a paired proof-feature callout to show breadth without giving every feature equal visual weight.

**Proof features**
- **Get air:** Venue panels + Staging · Customizable interface + Live map flexibility · Live monitoring + Sign-in and session control
- **Stay one-liners:** In-product support · Audit trails

**What NOT to cram**
- Separate customer and prospect versions
- Persona-specific subheads or use-case forks
- Full support-request workflow
- Sign-in methods and private-tunnel detail
- Any technical-detail catalog

**IT sidebar?**  
No. This option earns its dual-audience role by staying at the release-argument level; technical detail would compete with the central theme.

## Recommendation

**Option 2 — From intent to proof** is the strongest dual customer/prospect handout. Existing customers get recognizable changes and concrete proof, while prospects can understand the release argument without prior product knowledge; the stacked structure also gives the unequal pillar feature counts room without making one pillar feel smaller.
