Season timetable (not indefinite)

The series is a season, not a forever column. Default proposal:

  • Length: 8 weeks (tight) or 10 weeks (room for a mid-season + closing beat). Pick one when you lock the format.

  • Cadence: one series post every Tuesday, as Slot A of the Tue/Wed/Thu rhythm.

  • Prep: Friday before each posting week (same Friday prep loop).

  • After the last Tuesday: two-week pause. Review what performed, Mike’s comfort with the cadence, and whether Season 2 renews the same format, evolves it, or we replace it.

  • Start date: lock format + publish the week-by-week list first; Season 1 Week 1 = first Tuesday after Mike approves the opener batch. Don’t soft-launch without the full list written.

Recommendation. Start with an 8-week Season 1. Ten weeks is available on every option below if the first four posts land clean. Shorter seasons are easier to finish well than to pad.

The Shorter List

Week 2 of 8 · Equipment racks → one rack

Visual: Cropped row from the consolidation ledger — “5+ equipment racks” → “1 equipment rack.” Same template every Tuesday: ledger strip on light canvas, mint hairline, week counter top-right.

Most properties don’t add complexity all at once. They add it one rack at a time. Today: five racks serving five different systems. Shorter list: one rack. One orchestration platform. Every endpoint still reachable from one interface. The rack is evidence — not the headline. The variable you removed is split-brain operations: who owns which box, which remote, which vendor call. One interface. One team accountable for what’s on the screen.

Property Playbook

Week 1 of 8 · Build your static channel ring

Visual: Simple numbered tip card (no product UI screenshot): “1 Locals · 2 ESPN & majors · 3 Lock the ring · 4 Keep dynamic tuners for live packages.” Same card frame every Tuesday — off-white, Space Grotesk headline, Inter body.

Before you add another sports package, build your static channel ring. Start with locals — that’s what guests reach for first. Then ESPN and the major sports channels. Lock those tuners. Leave the rest dynamic for live events and premium packages. You’ll stop re-tuning the same twenty screens every Sunday, and your floor team will know exactly which channels never move. That’s not an A/V trick. It’s how you run a property where one team changes the channel once and every screen follows — from one interface.

Pillar 01 · Tip card · Sourced from Mike’s Consultation 06-26 · No client name.

The Question Before You Buy

Week 1 of 8 · Who owns the remote?

Visual: One bold question on light canvas — Space Grotesk, full width: “Who owns the remote?” Mint 3px rule under the series subhead. No photo; text is the hook.

Before you buy another control platform, ask your team one question: Who owns the remote when Marketing needs the message on the guest TVs, A/V is running the sportsbook schedule, and IT is troubleshooting the network? If the answer is three different people with three different remotes, you’re not buying a feature gap. You’re buying another variable. The property that wins doesn’t add another box. It aligns three teams on one interface.

Pillar 02 · Text-led · Dir/VP audience · Optional Mike quote on Thu slot, not in the series post itself.

What LUCI Controls

Week 3 of 8 · Pool deck & cabanas

Visual: Property map graphic with the pool zone highlighted (mint outline) — or anonymous pool-deck photo with a single zone callout. Not gear-forward. Same map frame every week; only the highlighted zone changes.

The pool deck isn’t separate from the property. It just feels that way when every cabana has its own remote. LUCI can run localized guest control at the cabana — pick a channel, keep it in that zone — while operations still sees every screen on the property map. Pool audio zones. Weather alerts to the deck speakers. The same channel discipline the sportsbook uses. One orchestration platform; the guest experience stays local where it should. Scope note: complex zone combining on divisible spaces runs through your existing Q-SYS backbone. LUCI is the interface your team — and your guests — actually use. One property. One map. Every endpoint accounted for.

How to pick

  • If brand clarity is the goal → A · The Shorter List (diagram-led, ledger-backed, hardest to confuse with commodity A/V content).

  • If “save this / send to my team” is the goal → B · Property Playbook (Mike’s real floor language; most standalone usefulness).

  • If LinkedIn audience fit is the goal → C · One Room, Three Teams (IT / marketing / ops leadership, not rack techs).

  • If you want the house triad on repeat → D · Control · Automate · Execute (already on website/deck).

  • If you want Mike’s voice in the frame → E · The Question Before You Buy (or Mike quotes on Thu thought-leadership, not inside the series).

  • If you want breadth education (“what runs through LUCI”) → F · What LUCI Controls (one property zone per week; map-led; see sample posts).

Combining A + B + F: Season 1 should still be one recognizable frame. Don’t rotate formats week to week. Better hybrid: What LUCI Controls as the name, Shorter List as the weekly close (“One zone. One fewer variable.”) or Property Playbook tips inside each zone post. Compare the four samples above, pick a letter (or hybrid), and 8 vs 10 weeks.
