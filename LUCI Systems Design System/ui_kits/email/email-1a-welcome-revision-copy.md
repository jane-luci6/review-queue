# Email 1A — Welcome revision

Copy only. No production template changes.

## Strategy

**What this email is and why**
A standalone welcome sent after contract and before travel is scheduled. It confirms the relationship, introduces the people behind it, and orients the reader without assigning them a place in the project.

**For whom**
A busy general manager, IT director, or operations leader who recently signed with LUCI and needs a quick reminder of what the partnership means.

**How to reach them**
Send from Michael Epstein at `mepstein@lucisystems.com`. Keep the note brief: personal welcome, one light line about LUCI, a neutral five-part project map, and a direct close from Mike.

**How to make an impact**
Sound like a CEO writing to a new partner, not a campaign explaining a product. The strongest proof is continuity: the engineers who learn the operation remain involved.

**Primary message**
Welcome to LUCI—you now have a real team that will learn your operation and stay with you.

---

## Direction 1 — The team that stays

**Angle**
Lead with LUCI’s most human point of difference: clients keep working with the same accountable team. The retention line works as proof of that commitment rather than a sales claim.

**Header headline**
Welcome to the LUCI family.

**Header subline**
A team that learns your operation—and stays with you.

**Body**

Dear #Name#,

I wanted to personally welcome you to the LUCI family. You chose us to bring your property’s A/V operation into one interface, and we’re glad to be part of your team.

The engineers you work with aren’t a handoff team. They’re the people who will learn your operation, build the system with you, and stay involved throughout the relationship.

We’ve never lost a client. I believe that comes down to something simple: we stay.

**Process lead-in**
For a simple overview, here’s how a LUCI project generally takes shape:

1. **Planning** — Align project scope, technical requirements, and responsibilities.
2. **System design** — Confirm network architecture and equipment requirements.
3. **Preparation** — Configure the LUCI appliance and coordinate site logistics.
4. **Onsite integration** — Connect A/V endpoints and validate system operation.
5. **Handoff** — Train your team and continue with ongoing support.

**Closing**

If anything comes up, reply to me. I’m always happy to help.

Welcome aboard.

Michael Epstein<br>
CEO

## Direction 3 — Meet your LUCI team

**Angle**
Put the engineers at the center immediately. This direction makes the welcome tangible by explaining who the client will work with and how that relationship carries across the project.

**Header headline**
You’re part of the LUCI family.

**Header subline**
The people behind your project are here to stay.

**Body**

Dear #Name#,

Welcome to LUCI. I want you to know what “family” means to us: real people who learn your operation and remain accountable for the work.

The engineers who design and integrate your system are the same people who stay involved after handoff. They’ll get to know your A/V environment, your team, and the way your property operates.

LUCI brings that operation into one interface. Our team makes sure the partnership around it remains just as connected.

**Process lead-in**
Every project is different, but the work follows five clear parts:

1. **Listen** — Understand the operation, scope, and technical requirements.
2. **Design** — Map the network, equipment, and system responsibilities.
3. **Prepare** — Configure the LUCI appliance and organize site needs.
4. **Integrate** — Connect A/V endpoints and test the full system.
5. **Support** — Train your team and remain engaged over time.

**Closing**

You’ll hear from the team directly as the work takes shape. My door is open, too.

Welcome aboard.

Michael Epstein<br>
CEO

## Recommendation

**Winner: Direction 1 — The team that stays**

It is the warmest and most credible welcome because it makes “family” mean something concrete: continuity and accountability. It borrows the cold-reader reminder from Direction 2 and the engineer focus from Direction 3, while keeping the benefit line to one sentence and the project map neutral.
