The result

The project was never
just one room
Casino technology rarely becomes complicated because of one project. It becomes complicated as each remodel, venue, and new system arrives with its own controls, vendors, and operating logic.
The LUCI team began the relationship by installing the LUCI platform at Ponca City. That first project put the control infrastructure and operating standard in place before any of the later expansions began. When the ballroom needed to come online, the team returned. When Swizzle and Swigs followed, each new phase extended the software already running the property.
That continuity changes the value of every project after the first. The software carries forward. The team carries the context. The next space joins a standard instead of adding another exception.

The latest project —
Swigs
Swigs put the relationship to work on a difficult build: a nearly 20-foot LED canvas that follows the room across three separate planes. Sixty cabinets and 480 panels had to align physically and map as one continuous 3840 × 1296 display.
A four-person onsite team, backed by the home office, completed the installation and primary programming in 25.5 working hours. The wall came online with four operating presets and seven independent video sources, while two new audio zones and the venue’s new endpoints joined technology already running through LUCI.
For the property team, the technical inventory resolves into choices that match the room: one full canvas, two seven-screen layouts, or a featured center image with supporting content on the outer sections. The venue changes modes. The operating standard does not.
