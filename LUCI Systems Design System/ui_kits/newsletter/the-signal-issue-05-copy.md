## 4. Design with LUCI

**Kicker:** 03 · Design with LUCI

**Headline:** Why our clients bring LUCI in early

**Highlight:** bring LUCI in early

**Dek:** The best time to define and plan how your A/V will function with LUCI is before rack locations, pathways, endpoints, and trade scopes are fixed.

**Body:**

Most of the decisions that determine how an A/V system will operate are made before the equipment arrives. That is also the period when architects, trades, installers, and operators are still aligning scopes and assumptions. When those teams are not working from the same operating plan, small gaps on the drawing set become lasting limits on how the A/V functions after opening.

Successful casinos bring LUCI into the early stages of planning so the operating model can guide those decisions while they are still easy to change.

### Before the infrastructure hardens

Early planning lets the operating model shape rack locations, landing rooms, cable paths, endpoints, speaker groups, and connections between trades. Those choices are easier to coordinate on a plan than after walls close and equipment locations are fixed.

### Before the handoffs multiply

A/V intent can blur as drawings move from consultant to architect, contractor, programmer, and commissioning team. Bringing LUCI in early keeps the chain shorter: the team planning the required infrastructure coordinates it through construction, system turn-on, and owner training.

### Bring LUCI in while the plan can still move

The scope can match the project—a tenant improvement, an LED-only design, or a fuller low-voltage and LED design for a larger addition. Contractors still install the hardware. LUCI defines the infrastructure the operating platform needs and carries that intent into the system the owner will use.

These planning and design services are included in the LUCI subscription.

## 5. Advice

**Kicker:** Inside LUCI

**Headline:** Set the operating structure before the devices arrive

**Dek:** A clean venue structure gives every endpoint a home and each person the right level of access.

**Body:**

In LUCI, displays, speaker zones, LED walls, and other controlled devices are organized by venue. Define those venues—and decide who can control them—before endpoints come online. The structure will be easier for your team to understand today and easier to extend later.

### Build venues first, then drop endpoints

Create every venue, save its views, and name each section first. When endpoints come online, place them inside a structure your team already recognizes. That keeps navigation clear and makes control easier to delegate by venue.

### Choose the access model on purpose

Choose whether one administrator controls everything or users receive access by location and source. Both models work. The important part is to decide deliberately before permissions grow by accident.

## 6. Quick Tip: calendar collision

**Kicker:** 04 · LUCI Quick Tip

**Headline:** What to do when two scheduled presets share the same TV

**Dek:** LUCI gives you a lot of ways to use presets—dayparts, drawings, live events, promotions, and recurring schedules. That flexibility is useful, but it also means we sometimes run into situations where a client has two scheduled presets unintentionally assigned to the same TV. Maybe they added a drawing to a TV that already follows a daypart, or an event runs into the next scheduled change.

**Intro:** When this happens, LUCI currently prioritizes the preset that starts later. But that may or may not be the result you want. To better plan for overlapping schedules, use these three checks to decide what should take priority and what the TV should return to afterward.

**Body:**

1. **Check the TVs.** Confirm which TVs each preset touches, including any screens shared across venues or event plans.
2. **Check the timing.** Compare the start time and duration of the daypart and the drawing so you know where they overlap.
3. **Plan the hand-back.** Decide what should run after the drawing and schedule or coordinate the return before the event begins.

## 7. What’s Coming: Reporting & Analytics + What’s New

**Kicker:** 05 · What’s coming

**Headline:** See the patterns behind day-to-day activity

**Dek:** The new version of LUCI is taking shape. Reporting & Analytics will turn day-to-day system activity into a clearer view of what is happening, what keeps happening, and where to look next.

**Body:**

We’re looking forward to putting these capabilities in your hands. Live monitoring shows what is happening now. Audit trails show who or what changed something and when. Reporting & Analytics adds the view across time, so your team can identify patterns and investigate the activity behind them.

- **Overview:** Scan incident volume and duration, command success and failure, latency, and preset activity across 24-hour, 7-day, and 30-day views.
- **Commands:** Trace individual commands by result and timing when a specific action needs a closer look.
- **Automation:** Review scheduled presets and automated workflows to see what ran, when it ran, and whether it completed.
- **Reliability:** Bring device incidents and system logs into view to identify endpoints that need attention.

Start with the broad pattern, then move into the relevant command, automation, or reliability record. Shift between the last 24 hours, 7 days, and 30 days to separate a one-time event from a recurring issue.

## 8. A Minute with Mike

**Kicker:** 06 · A Minute with Mike

**Headline:** Putting the power of programming in your hands

**Dek:** More day-to-day control for your team, with the LUCI partnership still behind the operation.

**Body:**

I use the phrase “the power of programming” because the people running a property should have more direct control over the system they use every day.

In the current version of LUCI, some configuration lives deep in the database. A property may know exactly what needs to change, but the work still has to route through our team or a third party. That can turn a straightforward operating change into a longer chain than it needs to be.

The new version brings more of that configuration forward. Clients and their integrators will be able to load their own maps and make more changes directly. The point is not to turn every operator into a programmer. It is to put the right tools in the hands of the people who understand the property, the room, and the work in front of them.

More control does not mean less partnership. Our team remains alongside yours to help plan, monitor, diagnose, and support the system. We are moving routine control closer to the operation while keeping the relationship and accountability behind it.

That principle runs through the new LUCI: give your team more ability to shape the rooms, views, data, and access it uses every day. The What’s New overview included with this issue is the first published look at where that direction is going.
