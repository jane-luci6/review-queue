## 4. Design with LUCI

**Kicker:** 03 · Design with LUCI

**Headline:** Keep the operating intent intact

**Dek:** The same team plans the infrastructure LUCI needs, coordinates it through construction, turns the system on, and trains the owner.

**Body:**

A/V design can lose its intent one handoff at a time. A consultant draws the system. A different team coordinates the building. Contractors interpret the plans. Then someone new arrives to turn everything on—and the owner has to trace the decisions backward when the finished room does not work as expected.

Design with LUCI keeps that chain shorter. Plan and design support is part of the LUCI service relationship, so the team designing the infrastructure already knows the operating platform it must support. We coordinate rack locations, endpoints, speaker groups, landing rooms, and connections with the other trades, then stay through system turn-on and owner training.

That continuity shapes the design itself. We plan around the infrastructure LUCI needs and minimize rack hardware from the start. The scope can fit the project: a tenant improvement, an LED-only design, or a fuller low-voltage and LED design for a larger addition. Contractors still install the hardware; LUCI carries the operating intent from the plans into the system the owner will use.

The result is fewer “who built this?” calls after opening. The people who planned how the system should work are still there when it is time to make it work—and when the owner begins running it.

## 5. Advice

**Kicker:** [ADVICE SECTION NAME — TBD]

**Headline:** Set the operating structure before the devices arrive

**Dek:** Two choices made early can keep control clear as the property grows.

**Body:**

### Build venues first, then drop endpoints

Create every venue, save the views, and name each section before endpoints come online. When the endpoints land, you can place them inside a structure the property already understands and delegate control cleanly by venue.

### Choose the access model on purpose

There is no harm in keeping control with one administrator everyone calls. Most clients create users freely, then filter each person’s access by location and source. Both models work; choose deliberately instead of letting access grow by accident.

## 6. Quick Tip: calendar collision

**Kicker:** 04 · LUCI Quick Tip

**Headline:** Know what wins before schedules overlap

**Dek:** Today, when two scheduled presets share the same TVs, the next preset wins. Don’t stack a drawing on a daypart without a plan.

**Body:**

1. **Check the TVs.** Confirm which TVs each preset touches, including any screens shared across venues or event plans.
2. **Check the timing.** Compare the start time and duration of the daypart and the drawing so you know where they overlap.
3. **Plan the hand-back.** Decide what should run after the drawing and schedule or coordinate the return before the event begins.

## 8. A Minute with Mike

**Kicker:** 06 · A Minute with Mike

**Headline:** Putting the power of programming in your hands

**Dek:** More day-to-day control for your team, with the LUCI partnership still behind the operation.

**Body:**

I use the phrase “the power of programming” because the people running a property should have more direct control over the system they use every day.

In the current version of LUCI, some configuration lives deep in the database. A property may know exactly what needs to change, but the work still has to route through our team or a third party. That can turn a straightforward operating change into a longer chain than it needs to be.

The new version brings more of that configuration forward. Clients and their integrators will be able to load their own maps and make more changes directly. The point is not to turn every operator into a programmer. It is to put the right tools in the hands of the people who understand the property, the room, and the work in front of them.

More control does not mean less partnership. Our team remains alongside yours to help plan, monitor, diagnose, and support the system. We are moving routine control closer to the operation while keeping the relationship and accountability behind it.

That principle runs through the new LUCI: give your team more ability to shape the rooms, views, data, and access it uses every day. The What’s New two-pager included with this issue is the first published look at where that direction is going.
