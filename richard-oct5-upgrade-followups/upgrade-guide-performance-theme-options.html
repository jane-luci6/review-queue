Why this ranking

Scheme 1 is the one that answers Jane’s fork and still obeys the lock. She asked for performance on its own before the pillars, or a rethink of the pillars. Before the pillars is the option that leaves “putting the power of programming in your hands” as the theme. Richard’s own order of operations matches that: the performance sentence is the standard line, and the major goal is that the customer can configure the site in the tool.

The band earns its place because one attested difference is easy to feel (the map) and one stability issue is visible enough that he stopped a demo for it (real-time degraded). A Guide that never mentions either will feel, to Jane, like it skipped the heart of the release. A Guide that builds a pillar out of them will feel, to the lock, like speed replaced programming.

Scheme 2 is the runner-up because it is the same fork — before, not rethink — with Reporting pulled up next to the claim. That honors her reason for putting analytics in this conversation: the views tell you what is happening and, via latency and incidents, something about how fast. The cost is mixture. Analytics is a place to look. It is not, on this transcript, proof that the release got faster. He never said latency improved. If scheme 2 is chosen, captions have to say “see latency and incidents over 24 hours, 7 days, and 30 days,” and stop there.

Scheme 3 loses because a co-equal pillar is a second theme, and because WebGL stops sitting with the map. Scheme 4 loses because it asks for speed sentences Richard did not say, on panels, staging, and sign-in. Scheme 5’s channel split is adopted, not chosen instead: the Guide gets the short honest condition, the two-pager gets a few felt lines, email is allowed to be warmer.

Risks

Ferrari, or “faster load,” without a figure

Both sound like a spec. Load time is Jane’s description of the release. It is not in the transcript. A customer who times the app and finds no change will trust the rest of the Guide less. The internal test is fair: does this release respond better in the hand. The public word for that, until Richard signs a number, is the map sentence he already said.

The condition outranks the control theme

If the band is louder than “programming in your hands,” or if scheme 3’s fourth pillar ships, performance becomes the theme. Richard ranked the configuration goal as the major one and performance as the standard line. Scheme 2 has the same risk if the before-block grows taller than the pillars.

Reporting opens on CoreX Health

He called CoreX Health nerdy and said nobody needs to live there. Overview, maybe, if they want it. Opening on health events teaches the section as an engineer console. Opening on incidents, downtime, presets, and latency teaches it as a way to run the property. Jane’s reason for including analytics — know what is happening, and how fast — is the operator reading, and it survives without the health tab in the lead.

“Fixed in the latest” copied straight into the Guide

He said the establishing-connection / real-time-degraded behavior is fixed in the latest build. The same meeting still showed that indicator, and a signed-in user missing from active sessions. Publishing “fixed” before he confirms the customer build advertises a demo the recording contradicts.

Control copy that reads as LUCI stepping back

“You don’t have to call Richard and Nathan” is the internal version of the theme. Printed, it says the partnership shrank. The external version is more of the everyday programming in the customer’s hands, with LUCI still behind the property. That sentence belongs to the control story, not to a speed banner.

WebGL as a replacement claim, or Audit mixed with Reporting

WebGL is how LUCI draws the map. It does not replace the property’s screens, walls, or controllers. Audit Trail is who changed what. Reporting is the roll-up. He separated them on the call. Mixing them sends people looking for charts inside Audit, or looking for the map story inside Analytics.

Open questions for Jane and Richard

  1. Measurements. Will Richard sign any number — load time, map responsiveness, command latency — and for which surface? Until he does, the Guide, the two-pager, and email stay qualitative.
  2. What “real-time” means externally. The degraded banner gone, session presence updating, dashboard incidents staying current, or all three? Is the fix in the build this Guide describes? If he will not confirm it this week, scheme 1 ships with the version-one line and the map pointer only.
  3. Version-one list. Is the general line enough, or is there a short list of cleaned-up issues he will stand behind in front of a customer? The panel-settings save failure and the CoreX audio glitch from this meeting are not that list.
  4. CoreX Health in the Guide. Subordinate line, or absent until operators are actually using it?
  5. The word “latency.” Fine for this audience (AV-side, his word), or does the section need “how long actions take” beside it?
  6. WebGL weight. Bullet on Live map, or its own card under View? Placement column is decided either way: View, not the performance band, not Reporting.
  7. Ferrari. Internal name only, or one email line Jane writes herself? Guide and two-pager stay without it.
  8. Pillar titles before anyone nests further. Confirm Guest experience / Your view / Security, or the benefit-led See and secure / Program and stage / Operate deeply. Performance placement does not change. Feature nesting labels will.
  9. Screenshots. He did not want “establishing connection” in the picture. Jane planned a fan of report views, not a full screen. Which operator views are safe to show?
  10. Partnership sentence. Who signs the external line for site configuration, so “more in your hands” cannot be read as reduced LUCI support?
