## Jane’s edit notes (apply to every piece)

1. **Cold-reader context at the top.** Someone may arrive from web search. Do not jump straight into the feature mid-conversation. Open with enough context that a first-time prospect knows: what kind of place this is about (property A/V / ops), what problem sits in the room, and why this piece exists — then move into the topic. Keep it short: 1–3 sentences of orientation, not a second essay.

2. **Lists / steps where they beat a paragraph.** Still lead with narrative. When a paragraph is really a sequence, a set of options, or a checklist of what they can do, turn it into bullets or numbered steps. Do not listify everything — only where the reader would suffer through a dense paragraph.

3. **Be specific. Active voice. Say what you mean.** Cut soft dancing and vague philosophy when a clear claim will do. Some soft guidance is fine; do not talk around the point. Prefer concrete actors and verbs (property staff / LUCI / the floor) over passive fog.
